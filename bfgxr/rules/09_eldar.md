# Eldar (MMS p.3-28)

## Faction Rules

This section uses the fan Eldar MMS v2.0 rules in place of XR's Eldar rules, which Ships of the Xenos allows as a complete replacement (Xenos p.19).

*Where XR's own Eldar rules differ, MMS takes priority. XR's version is noted after each rule as 'XR differs', for players using XR's Eldar.*

Source: *Eldar MMS v2.0* (fan ruleset; concept and rules Sigoroth, v2.0 by Roy Amkreutz), "Corsair and Craftworld Eldar Special Rules", pp.3-5. Used in place of XR's Eldar rules (Xenos p.19).

### Movement

The Eldar utilise solar sail technology specifically designed to make them ultra manoeuvrable and exceptionally fast. As such the Eldar movement is somewhat special:

- *Movement speed:* this is dependent on the Eldar's facing to the nearest point of the sunward table edge. Eldar have 3 speeds: the first is into the sun, the second is away from the sun and the third is abeam of the sunward edge. Eldar have no required minimum speed of movement and as such move any distance up to the determined movement speed.

- *Turning:* Eldar do not have to move a minimum distance before turning and can turn on the spot, but must make their turn at the start of their movement and cannot move, turn, move like other races. Their new direction of movement after this turn determines their movement speed.

- The Eldar are exceptionally quick and manoeuvrable and may move twice per movement phase, i.e. first turn, first move (at appropriate speed) and second turn, second move (at appropriate speed).

*XR differs (Xenos pp.16, 18): Corsair ships make their second move at the end of the ordnance phase, after both players have finished moving ordnance (not during the opponent's ordnance phase). Craftworld ships always make their second move immediately after the first ('Craftworld Rigging').*

*XR differs (Xenos p.16): before each normal move, an Eldar ship may make a free 45° turn away from the sunward table edge (unless already pointed directly away). It cannot solar jibe within a gravity well or while on Lock On.*

*XR differs (Xenos p.16): Eldar ships that do not move at least 5cm in total during their turn may be targeted as defences.*

*XR differs (Xenos p.16): an Eldar ship may initiate a boarding action during either of its moves, but only during its second move if it has not fired any weapons or launched any ordnance that turn. A ship that boards during its first move forfeits its second move.*

**Special Orders**

**Burn Retros:** The Eldar have no need for the *Burn Retros* special order and as such may not make use of it.

**All Ahead Full:** When on *All Ahead Full* special orders an Eldar ship redirects weapon energy to thrusters, gaining 5D6cm to one of its movements, and may not turn during both movement phases. Eldar may not ram.

*XR differs (Xenos pp.16, 18): Corsair ships may not use All Ahead Full or Burn Retros. Craftworld ships may use All Ahead Full, making a single move at twice their current Speed plus an extra 5D6cm.*

**Come to New Heading:** When on *Come to New Heading* special orders the Eldar player may make one extra turn at the end of their second movement. All other effects (halving firepower, etc) and special orders work as normal.

*XR differs (Xenos pp.16, 18): a Corsair ship on Come to New Heading makes its second move immediately after its first, instead of in the ordnance phase (all weapon penalties still apply). Craftworld ships cannot use Come to New Heading.*

**Lock On:** When on *Lock On* special orders the Eldar player may not turn in any movement phase.

### Leadership, Terrain & Damage

**Natural Spacefarers:** When generating leadership values, Eldar ships get +1 Ld, giving a range of 7-10. (This is the Eldar column of Appendix 4.)

**Celestial Phenomena:**

- Eldar do not suffer leadership or movement penalties for celestial phenomena.

- Eldar escorts automatically pass leadership tests for traversing asteroids, and Eldar capital ships re-roll any failed leadership tests when traversing asteroids.

- When attempting to traverse Warp Rifts the Eldar are assaulted by the Great Enemy Slaanesh and must take their leadership tests at -2 Ld. Furthermore, if they are 'Lost in the Warp' they will only find their way home on a 3+, being permanently lost on a 1 or 2.

*XR differs (Xenos p.16): Eldar get no general immunity to celestial phenomena and no asteroid bonus. Instead, when a solar flare occurs, each Eldar ship or squadron may take a leadership test; if passed, it turns directly away from the sunward edge and moves 2D6cm, ignoring the flare. Ships 'Lost in the Warp' suffer -1 to the roll to find their way home (MMS: home on 3+, lost permanently on 1-2).*

**Fragile:** Eldar vessels are built for speed and as such cannot take the kind of pounding that other races' vessels can. To reflect this, Eldar take critical hits on a 5+ rather than the regular 6+.

**Victory Points:** The standard Victory Points rules apply to the Eldar.

**Kinship:** In times of need Corsairs and Craftworlders alike can call upon their brethren for aid. To reflect this, the normal reserve rules are used. If a "Hero" is included in the fleet then the reserve ratio improves to 2:1. Furthermore, a Hero allows for the inclusion of the Void Dragon type vessel in the Corsair list at 0-1.

### Holofields

Against attacks which make use of the gunnery chart, the holofields force one extra right shift on the gunnery table, in addition to any other shift on the gunnery table. The holofield does not work under 15cm.

Against attacks which make no use of the gunnery table and target the ship directly (like lances, but not nova cannons or Armageddon guns), the holofield offers a save to represent the difficulty of targeting the Eldar vessel. Whenever an Eldar vessel is hit by such an attack, roll a D6 per hit and compare it to the holofield save. If the roll equals or exceeds the holofield save then do not place the blast marker; the holofield has thwarted the enemy sensors. If not, place the blast marker as normal; a shield has overloaded. The holofield keeps on working even if all shields have been overloaded. The holofield save depends on range: above 30cm, save on 5+; between 15-30cm, save on 6+; under 15cm, no save.

Against attacks which normally ignore holofields, like the star pulse generator or an activated Blackstone Fortress, the holofield offers no protection. Furthermore, holofields do not save against ordnance.

*XR differs (Xenos pp.16, 20, 26, 37): Eldar ships have no shields, only holofields, except that the Void Stalker, Wyrmship and Wild Serpent have Shields 1 from their wave shield. Instead of shields, 'wave shields' mean Eldar ships do not risk damage from blast markers or gas clouds.*

*XR differs (Xenos p.17): against gunnery, holofields give one extra right shift at any range, and each gunnery hit places a 'tracking' mark on the ship or squadron until the end of the turn (limit once per target per attacking ship or squadron). Against lances and ordnance (including hit-and-run raids), holofields give a 2+ save, worsened by 1 per tracking mark (to a maximum of 6+). Leadership tests to ram a holofield-protected ship are taken on an extra +2D6, and nova cannon scatter an extra 1D6cm even on a 'Hit'. Holofields give no protection against boarding, teleport attacks or catastrophic damage.*

### Turrets & Ordnance

**Turrets:** Eldar ships have superior targeting sensors and as such get +1 to their to-hit rolls.

**Ordnance:**

- *Stealth:* Enemy turrets suffer a -2 to hit when shooting at Eldar ordnance, usually only hitting on a 6+.

*XR differs (Xenos p.17): enemy turrets add +2 to the score needed to hit Eldar ordnance (except mines), to a maximum of 6+.*

- *Torpedoes:* Eldar torpedoes are equipped with superior targeting sensors, which means they must re-roll misses.

- *Bombers:* Eldar bomber pilots may re-roll the attack run dice.

- *Fighters:* Eldar fighters have a 4+ save against each wave they encounter (as opposed to against each marker, representing them being outnumbered). Each Eldar fighter gets to intercept once before any extra interceptions take place by surviving fighters.

*XR differs (Xenos p.17): Darkstar and Nightwing fighters have a 4+ resilient save.*

- *Boarding Torpedoes:* Eldar boarding torpedoes function in exactly the same way as normal boarding torpedoes, with the additional benefits of stealth (-2 to being hit by turrets) and a re-roll to hit.

- *Vampire Assault Boats:* Eldar assault boats have a speed of 30cm; they follow the standard rules for assault boats.

### Eldar Weapons

**Gunnery weapons:** Eldar batteries gain a left column shift on the Gunnery table.

**Pulsars:** When rolling to hit with pulsar lances, if a hit is scored you immediately make another attack. If the second attack is also a hit you now make a third attack. When on *Lock On* special orders only the first miss may be re-rolled.

*XR differs (Xenos p.16): on Lock On, any missed pulsar shot may be re-rolled, but no more dice than the attack's initial strength (MMS: only the first miss).*

**Phantom Lances:** Phantom lances function just like regular lances but score 1 hit on a roll of 4 or 5, and 2 hits on a roll of 6.

### Critical Hits

Eldar vessels use the Eldar Critical Hits Table (Appendix 3, p.225). Fragile: they take critical hits on a 5+ (Ghostships excepted).

### Optional: Dying Race

With your opponent's agreement:

- All capital ships have +2 hits.

- When reduced to 2 hits, Eldar capital ships may do nothing except attempt to disengage, moving towards the nearest celestial phenomena or table edge to do so (player's choice).

- Disabled Eldar ships may not fire or use any special orders except *Brace for Impact*.

- Disabled Eldar ships give 100% VPs to the opponent, and destroyed Eldar capital ships give +50% VPs.

- Disabled ships are ignored for target priority purposes and do not count their launch bays towards the fleet's attack craft limit.

- Disabled ships must try to avoid danger wherever possible: flying into an asteroid field to hide is fine, deliberately flying into an enemy torpedo salvo is not.

*XR differs (Xenos p.19): Ghostships do not gain the +2 hits, and capital ships are disabled at 2 or fewer hits.*

*Source: Eldar MMS v2.0, "Corsair and Craftworld Eldar Special Rules", pp.3-4*

### Commanders & Upgrades

**Corsair commanders** (MMS p.13):

- *Dragon Prince:* +1 in boarding. May be upgraded to "Hero" status for +50 pts.

- *Gryphon Knights:* ships led by Gryphon Knights may attempt a command check even if one has been failed this turn.

*XR differs (Xenos p.33): a Gryphon Knight gives +1 Ld to its assigned ship or squadron.*

**Craftworld commanders** (MMS p.22):

- Fleet commanders may not be placed aboard Ghostships.

- *High Admirals:* they and their elite bodyguard give +1 in a boarding action. A High Admiral may be upgraded to "Hero" status for +50 pts.

- *Farseers:* an Admiral may add additional Farseers to his vessel in order to gain more re-rolls; these re-rolls can be used fleet wide. Furthermore, you may include three extra Farseers, purchased at 15 points, in your fleet, each of whom must be assigned to a ship and gives that vessel a re-roll which may be used on that ship only. A Farseer may be placed aboard a Ghostship.

**Seer Council:** A Seer Council provides the High Admiral extra protection with its Foresight Power. Foresight Power allows the fleet commander's ship to go onto *Brace for Impact* (automatically) after the dice have been rolled to hit, gaining a 4+ retroactive save against each hit scored (re-roll criticals). The use of this power does not have to be declared before rolls to hit, and it may only be used once per battle. It can only be taken if the fleet has a High Admiral to lead it. Heroes cannot take Seer Councils.

*XR differs (Xenos p.18): any critical damage caused by the saved hits is discarded, and a new critical hit roll is made for each unsaved hit.*

**Aspect Warriors:** Any Craftworld capital ship may be given Aspect Warriors at +20 pts. Aspect Warriors grant +1 to the ship's boarding actions and all its hit-and-run attacks. Aspect Warriors may not be placed aboard Ghostships.

*XR differs (Xenos p.18): a vessel embarking aspect warriors has a +2 crew damage bonus and adds +1 to its hit-and-run attacks, and hit-and-run attacks made against it reduce their result by 1.*

*Source: Eldar MMS v2.0, Corsair and Craftworld fleet lists, pp.13, 22-23*

Spd lists three speeds: into the sun / away from the sun / abeam of the sunward edge (see Movement). Sh: H = holofield (see Holofields). Pulsar lances fire again after each hit (see Eldar Weapons). Corsairs launch Darkstar fighters (ASF, 30cm), Eagle bombers (BMR, 20cm) and Vampire Raiders (AB, 30cm); Craftworlds launch Nightwing fighters (ASF, 30cm), Phoenix bombers (BMR, 20cm) and Vampire assault boats (AB, 30cm).

**Arcs:** (L/F) and (R/F) are one mount each. (F) = one mount firing forward. Port and starboard pulsar lances on Craftworld ships still fire Front, as printed.

*XR differs (Xenos pp.20-37): every Eldar ship has a different XR profile and/or cost; most XR ships have no shields and fewer turrets, and most XR Corsair ships have lower Armour. Only the MMS profiles are given here.*

### Eldar Ship Special Rules

#### Void Dragon (MMS p.17)

The Void Dragon is a special ship, so you must have either a High Admiral in a Craftworld fleet or a "Hero" in a Corsair fleet. Whilst technically a Craftworld ship, the Void Dragon counts as belonging to the parent fleet as far as reserves are concerned. It counts as a Dragonship but doesn't require Wraithships to be taken.

#### Ghostships (MMS p.20)

The following special rules apply to Ghostships:

- Enemy vessels gain a +1 modifier when boarding or making hit-and-run attacks against a Ghostship.

- Ghostships may not initiate boarding actions or teleport attacks.

- Ghostships ignore the rule 'Fragile', thus taking critical hits on a 6+ as normal.

- A Ghostship which fails a special order may only move in the following turn.

*XR differs (Xenos pp.18, 34): up to three Dragonships or Wraithships may be converted into Ghostships at no extra cost, keeping their own profile. Ghostships gain +2 Hits, cannot equip launch bays or launch boarding torpedoes, and may always attempt a command check even if one has failed that turn; if a Ghostship fails a command check, it can do nothing except move and/or brace until the start of the opponent's next turn.*

#### Iyanden Fleet (MMS p.25)

- If led by Prince Yriel (represented by a High Admiral), Corsair vessels may be taken, counting Void Stalkers as Wyrmships, Supernovas as Void Dragons, and all other Corsair cruisers as Wraithships for the purpose of fleet limits. Corsair escorts may be taken freely in this case.

- Up to half of the capital ships in the fleet may be Ghostships.

- An Iyanden fleet (or any Eldar Corsair fleet) may include the Flame of Asuryan in place of a Dragonship or Void Dragon.

#### Craftworld Fleet Guidelines, not rules (MMS pp.24-26)

- *Biel-Tan:* Corsairs only as reserves, no Corsair capital ships. At least 75% of Dragonships should have the Aspect Warrior upgrade, and these Dragonships should have launch bays with assault boats. The fleet should be capital ship heavy: a maximum of about 25% of the fleet allowance should be spent on escorts. Ghostships should be rare: a maximum of one per three other capital ships.

- *Saim-Hann:* Corsairs and Craftworld vessels should be taken without restriction. Escorts should be common; mixed squadrons will represent the need to be 'all-rounders' (e.g. 1 Hellebore, 1 Aconite, 4 Nightshades). The Admiral must go on the most expensive ship. Fleet re-rolls should be rare. Capital ships should be rare; light cruisers should be much more common than Dragonships or Void Stalkers. Ghostships should be rare: a maximum of one per three other capital ships.

- *Ulthwé:* Corsairs only as reserves. A maximum of 25% of capital ships should have the Aspect Warrior upgrade. Re-rolls should be commonplace; buy as many Farseers as appropriate. Ghostships should be rare: a maximum of one per three other capital ships.

- *Alaitoc:* Corsairs should be taken without restriction, as should the Craftworld vessels. Over 2/3 of Corsair escort squadrons should be 'small' (two or three escorts). Make good use of the Hemlock; avoid using Nightshades. Capital ships should be rare; light cruisers should be much more common than Dragonships or Void Stalkers. Ghostships should be rare: a maximum of one per three other capital ships.

*XR differs (Xenos pp.35-37): the Biel-Tan, Saim-Hann, Ulthwé and Alaitoc fleet guidelines are binding rules in XR, with some different details:*

*Biel-Tan: Corsair capital ships not allowed even as reserves; Dragonships with aspect warriors must also carry Vampire assault boats or boarding torpedoes.*

*Saim-Hann: at least as many escort squadrons as capital ships, and "These squadrons must include at least two different types of escort"; the commander may buy no more than one extra re-roll.*

*Ulthwé: as many re-rolls and Farseers as possible.*

*Alaitoc: no more Nightshades than Hemlocks; no more capital ships than escort squadrons.*

#### Eldar Transport (XR, Xenos p.31)

An Eldar transport is a convoy ship and will follow all the normal rules for fleet support units (and convoy ships) described in the main rulebook. It has a transport capacity of 1, and will benefit from all applicable Eldar special rules. Eldar transports may *only* be included when the chosen scenario imposes a transport capacity requirement on your fleet.

#### Haven Class Spire (XR, Xenos p.32)

A Haven Spire is a high orbit defence choice for any Eldar fleet. It follows the standard rules for fleet support units as described in the main rulebook, except where modified below:

**Leadership:** A Haven Spire has a nominal leadership value of 8. However, a fleet's commander may lead the battle aboard the Haven Spire (even if normally forced to embark somewhere else), allowing it to use his or her leadership instead.

**Docking:** Eldar ships can dock (or undock) with a Haven Spire in base contact by passing a leadership test. Ships that are docked cannot attack or launch ordnance, though holofields continue to work normally, and they can attempt special orders if desired. Vessels that remain docked with the station for one full turn gain +2D6 to repair critical damage in the end phase, and are considered to have reloaded ordnance for free. The station may also apply its repair bonus to itself if there are no ships docked with it at any point during its turn.

**Webway Portal:** Many Eldar Havens are situated in deep space or near one of the Exodite Worlds, usually near a Webway Portal large enough for starships to traverse. The owning player may use the Webway to summon additional warships to its aid. If the fleet includes an Eldar Haven, it may take both Craftworld and/or Corsair Eldar ships as reserves at 50% of their normal cost. However, any such ships *must* start the game off the table. Beginning on turn 2, after the Eldar fleet moves, roll a D6. On a 5+, one reserve capital ship or escort squadron (owner's choice) appears along the table edge closest to the Eldar Haven. These reserve ships cannot move in the same turn they arrive.

Note that if a fleet is led by a Hero of the Eldar, the bonus to the fleet's reserves ratio also applies to ships purchased through the Webway Portal.

**Hidden Base:** An Eldar Haven, even more so than other clandestine redoubts, relies more on its ability to remain hidden than any capacity to repulse a sustained assault. As a result, Haven Spires *cannot* be used in any battlezone from the Flare Region to the Inner Biosphere.

When playing in a campaign, an Eldar fleet may start with a free Haven Spire in their fleet list, and may earn up to two more via special appeal roll (note that any that are lost still count against this limit). To do so, they must have 11+ renown and score 5+ on the appeal roll to succeed. Each Haven Spire on a player's campaign fleet list counts as a hidden base (see the Build-Up section of the campaign rules). In individual battles, the cost of any Haven Spire that participates must still be counted normally as part of the chosen forces.

When operating as a hidden base, if an Eldar Haven is revealed and survives the initial attack, the owning player can attempt to retreat the station back into the Webway to prevent further assaults on it. To do so, the owner rolls a D6 after the battle. On a roll of 1 the Haven Spire is lost in the Webway and must be struck from the roster. On a 2+, it has successfully traversed the Webway to a new system and must once again be located by an opponent before it can be attacked.

*XR differs (Xenos pp.31-32): the Haven Spire cannot move, and has a Netherfield and a Polarization Field.*

#### Booklet Hybrid Options (from XR)

These options are taken from XR's Eldar rules for use with the MMS ships; they are not part of MMS v2.0.

- *Vampire Hunters* (Xenos pp.17, 20, 27-28): a Void Stalker, Void Dragon, or Dragonship with launch bays may add Vampire Hunters (torpedo bombers, TB 20cm) to its launch bays for +40 pts. Their torpedoes must re-roll missed attacks, like all Eldar torpedoes.

- *Fusion and distortion torpedoes* (Xenos pp.17, 20, 26; follow the rules for melta and vortex torpedoes): a Void Stalker may take fusion or distortion torpedoes for +25 pts, as an alternative to boarding torpedoes. A Wyrmship may arm both keel torpedo launchers with fusion or distortion torpedoes for +50 pts.

- *Mine launchers* (Xenos pp.33-34): up to two cruisers in the fleet may replace all of their launch bays with mine launchers for +5 pts per bay replaced. Mines do not benefit from stealth.

*XR differs (Xenos p.21): XR also offers the Supernova's Prismatic Array, which is not part of MMS.*
