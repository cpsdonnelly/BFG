# Core Rules

## 1. Basics

**Measuring:** a ship occupies the point marked by the stem of its base. Measure moves from the stem, and ranges (for weapons and other ship-to-ship effects) from stem to stem. You may always measure before deciding, unless all players agree otherwise.

**Base contact:** a ship's base represents close range around it. Anything that affects an area of the table, such as ordnance or celestial phenomena, affects a ship whose base touches it.

**Passing other ships:** the flat table stands in for three dimensions, so ships can move and fire past each other freely, with no risk of collision.

**Re-rolls:** pick up the dice and roll them again. The second result stands, even if it is worse, and cannot be re-rolled again by any means.

**Unit types:** every unit is a battleship, cruiser, escort or defence. Battleships and cruisers together are capital ships. Defences with 2 or fewer starting hits follow the rules for escorts, and those with 3 or more follow the rules for capital ships, except where a rule says otherwise (such as the Gunnery Table, which has its own Defences column).

**Comparing size:** battleships are larger than cruisers, and cruisers are larger than escorts. A defence is larger than anything with fewer starting hits and smaller than anything with more.

**Ship profiles:** the roster tables give each ship's characteristics:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pts | Points cost. |
| Hits | Damage the ship can take before it is reduced to a wreck. Its type (battleship, cruiser, escort or defence) is given by its roster heading or Class cell. |
| Base | Base size (section 4). |
| Spd | How far it moves in its movement phase (section 10). |
| Turn | How far it can turn when it turns: 45° or 90°. |
| Sh | Shields: hits blocked before they go down (section 11). |
| Armr | The D6 score an attacker needs to hit it. A separate value for the front (marked P for an armoured prow) applies to shots from its front arc. |
| Tur | Turrets: dice rolled to shoot down attacking ordnance (section 28). |
| Armament | Each weapon's range (or speed, for ordnance), firepower (FP) or strength (S), and fire arc. |

Leadership (Ld) is not printed: it is rolled before each game (section 3), or set by the campaign or a commander. You may use any models you like, but tell your opponent which ship each model represents.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "First Principles" and "Unit Types", pp.3-6*

## 2. The Turn

Players take turns. In your turn you move all your ships, then shoot with them, then both players move their ordnance, then the end phase is played. A **game turn** is both players' turns together (a battle of eight game turns has sixteen player turns); 'turn' on its own always means one player's turn.

**First turn:** both players roll a D6, and the higher score chooses whether to go first or second, unless the scenario decides (in an ambush, for example, the attacker always goes first).

**Ending the battle:** as the scenario says. Usually the battle continues until one side surrenders or has no ships left on the table. Some scenarios allow a sudden-death victory (such as destroying the enemy flagship), and players may agree a time limit.

**Acting out of turn:** some actions happen in the opponent's turn, such as Brace for Impact, and an event can interrupt a turn. Afterwards, the turn continues as normal.

### Turn Sequence

> 1\. **Movement phase:** remove the special order markers placed on your ships in your last turn (Brace for Impact markers are removed at the end of this turn instead). Then move your ships and squadrons one at a time, taking any command checks for special orders before each one moves. Once a command check fails, no more may be taken this turn (sections 6, 7 and 10).
>
> 2\. **Shooting phase:** fire with your ships, one at a time, and launch ordnance at the end of the phase (sections 13-15).
>
> 3\. **Ordnance phase:** both players move and attack with all ordnance on the table, including ordnance launched in earlier turns. The player whose turn it is moves all of theirs first (sections 15-30).
>
> 4\. **End phase:** resolve boarding actions and teleport attacks, then both players attempt to repair critical damage, then the player whose turn it is removes blast markers (sections 31, 32 and 36).

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Turn" and "Turn Sequence", pp.10-11; "The End Phase", pp.34-35*

## 3. Leadership (incl. Faction Tables)

In Battlefleet Gothic, the expertise of a captain and his (or her) crew is shown by the ship's leadership value: the higher it is, the better the captain and crew. Leadership is very important, because ships must test against this value if they wish to use special orders.

As mentioned earlier, in one-off games you roll a D6 to determine each ship's leadership value before the start of the game. Look up the result of the dice roll on the table below to see what Leadership value the ship has. Escorts roll once per squadron, with the whole squadron sharing the value rolled. Each capital ship rolls individually for leadership, even if it is part of a squadron.

**Starting Leadership by faction:** see Appendix 4, p.168.

### Leadership Modifiers

During a game, various modifiers may be applied to your ships that may increase or decrease their leadership value. However, under no circumstances can a ship's leadership be modified higher than 10 after taking all bonuses and penalties into account. In effect, a leadership check roll of 11 or 12 always fails unless specifically stated otherwise.

If a ship is reduced to leadership 1 or less, it cannot voluntarily attempt special orders or leadership tests of any kind unless some combination of effects brings it to leadership 2 or greater. If forced to take a leadership test with leadership 1 or less, the ship automatically fails. Note that a ship's leadership value cannot be reduced below zero.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Introduction - Leadership", p.7*

### Faction-Specific Leadership Tables

Some factions do not use the default Starting Leadership table; their fleet lists replace it. All of them are unified into one table in the Appendix: see Appendix 4, p.168. Factions not listed there use the Default column.

*Source: BFG:XR faction fleet lists / codex documents*

## 4. Assembling the Fleet

**Points:** agree a points limit. Each player picks ships, adding up their points, up to that limit. Some scenarios give the sides different or random points, and say so.

**Fleet lists:** each faction's fleet list says which commanders, capital ships, escorts, ordnance, upgrades and reserves it may take, and its attack rating (if none is given, use the default in the Scenarios section). The number of larger capital ships (battleships, grand cruisers, battlecruisers) is often limited by the number of ordinary or light cruisers taken. Escorts are usually unlimited unless the list says otherwise.

**Writing your list:** write your fleet list down, with all refits and points, and have a model for every ship. Lists are normally open. If both players agree, lists may stay secret until the end of the game (or campaign), but if either player asks to see the other's list, both are revealed immediately.

**Ship Class Variants:** In some ship entries, you may see options to mount different weaponry than 'regular' models of that ship or class. For example, some Lunar class cruisers may exchange their prow torpedoes for a nova cannon. Unless specifically noted as unique in their profile, such as the mighty Planet Killer, you may have multiple copies of any such ship or class variant in your fleet list (subject to the normal restrictions given in their profiles).

**Playing without fleet lists:** regular opponents may agree to ignore some or all fleet list restrictions (an all-battleship battle, say), as long as every player agrees.

**Base sizes:** large bases are 60mm across and small bases 32mm. A ship with 3 or more shields or more than 10 hits uses a large base; every other ship uses a small one. Any capital ship may take Tractor Fields for free to use a large base: it becomes easier for it to ram and board, but it is a larger target for ordnance, ramming and boarding.

The rules for forming squadrons are in section 8.

### Allies of Convenience

Ships from outside your faction, whether from your fleet list, taken as reserves or given by the scenario, are allies of convenience unless stated otherwise. Unless their fleet list allows it, allies of convenience:

- may only be in squadrons with ships of their own faction;

- may only have the refits shown in their own profiles;

- cannot carry commanders from the main fleet, or use those commanders' re-rolls.

**Factions:** for deciding who is an ally of convenience, the factions are as below. Sub-factions are always full allies of others in the same faction, but some special rules refer to them separately.

### Reserves

- For every 3 battleships, 3 cruisers (of any sub-type) or 3 escorts chosen from your fleet list, you may take 1 ship of the same type from a fleet list named in your list's reserves. Reserves cost points as normal, and reserves from another faction are allies of convenience.

- Ships that are already allies of convenience in the list they come from (such as Space Marine strike cruisers in an Imperial Navy Armageddon Sector fleet) cannot be taken as reserves.

- You cannot take more copies of a class as reserves than its own fleet list could field. Reserves count towards your fleet's limits as normal; their own list's other restrictions are waived.

- You may take reserves from any number of your allowed lists, but reserves never qualify you for more reserves or further reserve lists. For example, taking 9 escorts from your own list and 3 from a reserve list does not let you take a reserve from that reserve list's reserves.

- Reserves from your own faction may take any refits available to them in their profile, their own fleet list or the list they join. Commanders may only come from your main fleet list.

- Ships and characters awarded by a scenario or campaign ignore the reserve restrictions.

**Alternative reserves:** if both players agree beforehand, fleets may take reserves from lists other than those their fleet list allows. Players are encouraged (as a guide, not a restriction) not to take them from their faction's natural enemies, or from fleets that include them. ### Fleet Detachments

A fleet detachment is a small fleet list that cannot be used on its own: it joins a fleet list named in its description (the joined fleet). A fleet may be joined by several different detachments, but never by two copies of the same one. A detachment cannot join another detachment, but two detachments joining the same fleet count each other as the joined fleet for list options (unless they are restricted as allies of convenience).

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Assembling the Fleet", pp.57-60*

## 5. Fleet Commanders

Your fleet may be led by a commander, such as an Imperial Admiral, Chaos Warmaster, Eldar Dragon Prince or Ork Warboss. A fleet commander is a unique type of unit that is not represented by a separate model. Instead, a commander must embark upon a ship in your fleet, which will usually be a flagship of some sort. That ship will then act as the game piece for your fleet commander (in addition to its normal rules).

Fleet commanders often come with special rules that will benefit their own ship or those around it. In addition, they will often have a fixed leadership value (shown in brackets). A fleet commander's leadership always supersedes that of the vessel he or she is assigned to, even if it is lower!

Lastly, most fleet commanders may purchase special upgrades for themselves or their ship, including re-rolls for your fleet's command checks. The cost of a fleet commander (and any of his or her re-rolls and upgrades) is added to that of the assigned ship - if it is lost or crippled, that cost will count against the vessel's victory point value (see the Scenarios section for details).

A fleet of more than 750 points must be led by a fleet commander; if the commander cannot embark on an escort, the fleet must also include at least one capital ship. Smaller fleets may have a commander if you wish.

A commander's special rules combine with the special rules and upgrades of their ship, unless they match or contradict each other.

**Secondary commanders:** some fleets may take these. They work exactly like fleet commanders, but cannot be the required commander of a fleet over 750 points, and may have restricted re-rolls or upgrades.

No capital ship or escort squadron may carry more than one commander of any kind.

**Fleet Commander Re-rolls:** most commanders have command re-rolls, as listed in their fleet list. Each can be used once per game, to re-roll a failed command check or leadership test for any ship or squadron in the fleet. Only one re-roll can be used on a single test. If the commander's ship suffers a Bridge Smashed critical hit or is destroyed, the commander's re-rolls are lost for the rest of the battle.

**Note also:** during a radiation burst, Fleet Commanders may only use their re-rolls on command checks for their own ship or squadron.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Assembling the Fleet - Fleet Commanders", p.57*

## 6. Command Checks & Modifiers

In the midst of battle a captain may order his ships to perform special maneuvers or direct more of his ship's power to weapons or engines. However, a vessel's crew is never perfect; miscommunication, mistakes, and slow response can prevent a ship from enacting its captain's orders exactly as instructed. To represent this uncertainty, a vessel undertaking special orders must first pass a Command check.

A command check is resolved by rolling 2D6 and comparing the result to the ship's leadership (Ld). If the roll is equal to or less than the ship's Ld value, it has passed the check and goes onto special orders. Other than Brace for Impact, all special orders last until the start of the vessel's next turn.

If the roll is higher than the ship's Ld value, it fails the check and may not go onto special orders. Furthermore, once you have failed a command check for one ship in your fleet, you may not make any further command checks to use special orders in the same turn. It can be imagined that the delays and confusion in trying to get that particular ship to perform special orders means that the opportunity has passed for any further orders to be issued.

With all special orders except Brace for Impact you must attempt the command check in the movement phase, before moving. Remember that a ship must then resolve any required movement immediately, and no ship may be on multiple special orders at the same time unless specifically stated in its rules.

### Command Check Modifiers

Some circumstances will make it easier or harder for a vessel to use special orders. To represent this, there are two modifiers that can apply to command checks, one positive and one negative. Remember that these modifiers cannot increase a vessel's Ld value above 10 or below zero.

Under Fire (-1 Ld): If a ship has blast markers in contact with its base, it is under fire and suffers a -1 penalty to its leadership value when attempting command checks.

Enemy Contacts (+1 Ld): Enemy ships operating under special orders create a variety of anomalous energy readings and sensor echoes. This makes it much easier for enemy ships to detect them and react. A vessel gains a +1 bonus to its leadership value when attempting command checks if any enemy ships are on special orders.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Introduction - Special Orders / Command Checks", p.8-9*

## 7. Special Orders

There are six different special orders that a ship may use, each one allowing the vessel to perform better at something, such as gunnery or navigation, during its turn. A ship or squadron may only ever be on one special order at a time, unless specifically stated otherwise in its special rules.

Special orders are declared during the movement phase by choosing a vessel or squadron, declaring the order, resolving the associated command check, then moving the ship or squadron. You must resolve this process completely, including any movement, before continuing with the next ship or squadron.

**All Ahead Full:** the ship adds 4D6cm to its speed and must move the full distance. It may not turn, and its macrobatteries and lances fire at half firepower and strength (rounding up). A squadron rolls once, and every member adds the result. If members roll different numbers of dice, roll the most dice any member is allowed; members allowed fewer use the highest results, up to their own number (a ship that rolls 2D6 uses the two highest of the 4D6 rolled). Ramming: section 12.

**Come to New Heading:** the ship may make one extra turn during its move. The normal restrictions apply to it, so a capital ship must move its minimum straight distance again before its second turn (a cruiser that moved 10cm before turning must move another 10cm). Its macrobatteries and lances fire at half (rounding up). Ponderous ships cannot use this order.

**Burn Retros:** the ship does not need to move a minimum distance or move straight before turning, and may stay still, but may move no more than half its speed. Its macrobatteries and lances fire at half (rounding up).

**Lock On:** in the shooting phase the ship may re-roll any missed to-hit dice for its lances and gunnery weapons (the second result stands). It may not turn during its move.

**Reload Ordnance:** all of the ship's ordnance, of every type, is reloaded and may be launched in any later shooting phase. Its movement and shooting are unaffected. Every ship starts the game with its ordnance loaded unless stated otherwise.

**Brace For Impact:** The second combat-oriented special order is Brace For Impact, which protects the ship from damage. It is unique among special orders in that it may be used any time the ship faces damage (but before the result is rolled).

**Further Brace for Impact notes:** If the ship passes its command check, it is placed on Brace For Impact special orders until the end of its next turn, replacing any special orders it is on currently. A braced ship gains a saving throw against any hits it suffers (but not hits against its shields or other defences). Roll a D6 for each hit: on a score of 4+, the hit is ignored. A ship on Brace For Impact orders may not use special orders at all during its next turn. The ship also halves the firepower of its gunnery weapons and the strength of both its lances and ordnance armaments (rounding up).

**Declaring Brace For Impact Orders:** declare it after any checks that decide whether an attack happens, but before any combat rolls for that attack (rolls to hit, scatter, turrets, weapon strength or critical damage). For example, against ramming or an asteroid field you can wait for the result of the Leadership test, and against an exploding ship you can wait until its 3D6cm blast radius is measured. A ship decides separately against each of an attacker's weapons (it may brace against an attacker's lances after not bracing against its gunnery), but may attempt to brace only once per attacker per turn. If the command check fails, it cannot try again until the ship, squadron or ordnance marker attacking it has finished all its attacks.

**Other Special Orders:** Any changes to a ship's movement resulting from previous special orders remain in effect even after bracing (such as from All Ahead Full or Burn Retros orders). Similarly, a vessel that is braced while on Reload Ordnance orders is still reloaded normally.

**Bonus Damage:** Brace For Impact special orders do not protect against any critical damage inflicted by unsaved hits. However, against any weapons which inflict multiple damage points with each hit (such as some Ork and Eldar weapons), a braced ship rolls one save per damage point inflicted past its shields and other defences.

**Boarding & Hit-and-Run Raids:** Braced ships may save against any hit-and-run raids directed at them, just like other types of attack. (The target chooses whether to brace before the attack is rolled; if it passes the command check, it saves against each raid on a 4+.) However, Brace For Impact special orders offer no protection whatsoever against any damage (critical or otherwise) inflicted during a boarding action.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Shooting Phase - Brace For Impact", p.21 (summary box p.9)*

### Command Checks

A vessel undertaking special orders must first pass a Command check. A command check is resolved by rolling 2D6 and comparing the result to the ship's leadership (Ld). If the roll is equal to or less than the ship's Ld value, it has passed the check and goes onto special orders. Other than Brace for Impact, all special orders last until the start of the vessel's next turn.

If the roll is higher than the ship's Ld value, it fails the check and may not go onto special orders. Furthermore, once you have failed a command check for one ship in your fleet, you many not make any further command checks to use special orders in the same turn.

With all special orders except Brace for Impact you must attempt the command check in the movement phase, before moving. There is no restriction on how many ships and/or squadrons in your fleet may be on special orders at one time.

If your fleet has access to special rules that grant the ability to automatically pass a command check, such abilities may only be used before a command check is failed unless specifically stated otherwise.

**Command Check Modifiers:** Under Fire (-1Ld): If a ship has blast markers in contact with its base, it is under fire and suffers a -1 penalty to its leadership value when attempting command checks.

Enemy Contacts (+1Ld): A vessel gains a +1 bonus to its leadership value when attempting command checks if any enemy ships are on special orders.

(These modifiers cannot increase a vessel's Ld value above 10 or below zero. The Under Fire and Enemy Contacts modifiers apply to command checks only, not to other leadership tests.)

**Other leadership tests:** tests other than command checks (for target priority, ramming, navigating an asteroid field and so on) are taken the same way: roll 2D6, and pass on a score equal to or below the ship's Ld. A ship may take several leadership tests in a turn, even after a command check has failed, but may not take the same test more than once. Under Fire and Enemy Contacts do not apply to them; other Ld modifiers may, as their rules say.

A summary of all six special orders is given in Appendix 6, p.170.

## 8. Squadrons

### Forming Squadrons

**Capital Ships:** Squadrons of capital ships will be formed at the beginning of a game, before any ships are deployed for battle. See the 'Forming up the Fleet' section on page 56 for further details regarding capital ship squadrons.

**Escorts:** Squadrons of escorts, on the other hand, are selected as such from your fleet list at the time players pick their forces. Usually, escort squadrons will have no more than six ships, although some fleet lists may be different (again, see page 56 for more details).

In general, squadrons may include ships of different classes, as long as they are all the same type (and no more than four are 2-hit escorts). For example, you could form an escort squadron of three Cobra class destroyers and three Firestorm class frigates. In practice, it is often easier to form squadrons of a single class, but this is ultimately a matter of taste.

### Squadron Leadership

Using squadrons enables the Admiral of the Fleet to give orders to groups of ships at the same time, instead of having to relay orders to each ship in his fleet individually. Furthermore, squadrons of ships are often experienced at fighting together, and thus will be less prone to miscommunication and errors. These advantages are represented by the following special rules:

**Capital Ship Squadrons:** Each capital ship in a squadron rolls for leadership individually, as normal. However, if a capital ship squadron contains ships with different leadership values, the squadron will always use the highest leadership value among its remaining members.

**Escort Squadrons:** Before the start of the game, each escort squadron only makes a single roll to determine the leadership value for the entire squadron as a whole.

**Command Checks & Other Rolls:** All squadrons have the highly desirable advantage of only needing to attempt a single command check to place the entire squadron on special orders. However, squadrons under fire suffer -1 Ld to their command checks if any one member is in contact with blast markers. All other leadership modifiers apply to ships in the squadron normally. As is true for individual vessels, squadrons may only undergo one special order per turn (including Brace For Impact, which replaces any current special orders for the entire squadron).

Any other rolls that ships would be required to make are also resolved once for the whole squadron, such as for bonus speed while on All Ahead Full special orders, leadership tests, target priority checks, and so forth, unless otherwise specified.

### Squadron Coherency

To remain part of a squadron, each member must be no more than 15cm away from another ship in the squadron, so that they form a continuous chain with 'links' of up to 15cm between each ship.

If a ship is forced to break formation, it no longer counts as part of the squadron until it moves back to within 15cm of another member. If a squadron breaks into multiple groups, simply designate one as the 'main body' of the squadron that the others must rejoin, and treat the rest as out of formation. Any ships out of formation must move to restore squadron coherency as soon as possible, and cannot benefit from the squadron's special orders until they have done so.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Advanced Rules - Squadrons", p.43*

### Maneuvering Squadrons

Individual ships in a squadron move freely using the standard movement rules, as long as they stay in formation at the end of their move. If any ship in an escort squadron moves off the table edge, the entire squadron must immediately, and in later turns, only attempt to disengage. Capital ships disengage individually as normal.

### Shooting at Squadrons

**Target aspect:** ships in a targeted squadron may present different aspects to the attacker (for example, some closing and some abeam). The attacker chooses which aspect to fire at, but cannot score hits on any ship that is harder to target than that category. Against two closing and two abeam ships, firing at them as closing targets can only hit the two closing ships.

**Allocating hits:** hits go to the nearest eligible ship in the squadron until it is destroyed (including any critical damage), then the next nearest eligible ship takes the remaining hits, and so on. Ships out of the attacker's range or fire arc cannot be hit, and a Leadership test cannot be used to pick out an individual ship in a squadron.

This does not apply to ordnance, which still attacks the first ship it contacts as normal. Likewise, boarding actions, teleport attacks and so forth may pick out individual targets as per their rules, and a nova cannon only damages ships actually under the template.

**Critical damage before destruction:** a ship in a squadron that is fired on until destroyed must roll for and apply all its critical damage before it counts as destroyed, so a squadron cannot 'hide' extra damage on a wrecked ship. The easiest way is to roll for critical hits one damage point at a time, recording any extra damage as you go.

For example, a squadron of three Dauntless light cruisers (6 hits and 1 shield total) is fired upon and takes a total of 13 hits. All critical damage rolls must be made for the first Dauntless before declaring it destroyed: if, while applying hits one at a time, it rolls Thrusters Damaged (+1 damage) and Engine Room Damaged (+1 damage) as it is destroyed, eight hits remain to be applied to the next closest Dauntless.

This matters most for squadrons of ships that do not roll critical damage normally, such as escorts, small defences and other unique ships that take a point of damage when they suffer a critical hit.

**Mixed armour:** if the ships in the target squadron have different Armour values (or present arcs with different values), roll against the lowest Armour value. Every die that equals or beats it scores a hit: allocate the dice one at a time, lowest rolls first, each to the nearest ship it can hit.

### Shooting by Squadrons

Ships in a squadron add their firepower or strength together before rolling. When a squadron's shooting is halved (when braced, for example), escorts total their firepower first and then halve it (rounding up); capital ships still halve individually.

Squadrons follow the normal rules for target priority, line of sight and so on. Members out of range or arc of the main target may fire at another enemy, just as one ship can fire different arcs at different enemies.

**Different aspects or ranges:** if members see the target from different aspects or at different ranges, work out each group's firepower separately, then combine the dice for each Armour value the target presents into one attack per Armour value, and apply all the hits together. For example, five frigates fire at a cruiser with 6+ prow armour and 5+ elsewhere: one behind it (moving away) and three abeam combine their dice against 5+, and the one in front rolls separately against 6+.

**Squadron against squadron:** each group of attackers that sees a different mix of target aspects chooses which aspect to fire at (Shooting at Squadrons, above). Groups firing at the same set of target ships, needing the same scores to hit, combine their dice into one attack. Groups firing at different sets of targets, or needing different scores because the targets' Armour differs, resolve separately, in any order, and blast markers placed by one group do not affect the others. A group can be a single ship. Always try the normal squadron rules first: these steps are only needed when facings and Armour values differ.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Advanced Rules - Squadrons", pp.44-47*

**Forming Squadrons** (from "Assembling the Fleet - Building Your List", p.58)

When forming your ships into squadrons, your fleet must obey the following restrictions, except where your faction's fleet list states otherwise:

All escort ships must be formed into squadrons of between two and six ships each, at the time they are chosen for your fleet. The escorts in a squadron do not have to all be of the same class, but you may not have more than four 2-hit escorts in one squadron.

A single escort may be taken in a fleet as long as there are no other escorts included. However, you cannot ignore any other restrictions, such as fleets which require a minimum number of escorts, and so forth. In addition, if a fleet has at least two escorts, they must be divided so that no escort is operating without at least one other in a squadron (except those that are normally allowed to operate alone, such as transports or Tau Messenger ships).

Cruisers, battlecruisers, heavy cruisers and light cruisers may be formed into squadrons of two to four ships each, at the time they are deployed, and may be mixed in the same squadron.

Grand cruisers and battleships may be formed into squadrons of two to three ships at the time they are deployed, but you may not mix battleships and grand cruisers in a squadron.

Note that the distinctions between different types of cruisers will be found in the fleet list itself - as far as profiles go, they are all cruisers (also note that some fleets will use different names for their cruisers - look for the terms grand, heavy, light, etc). If a fleet does not differentiate its cruisers, they will use the preceding rules for normal cruisers.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Assembling the Fleet - Building Your List", p.58*

## 9. Ship Orientation & Fire Arcs

### The Bearing Compass

A vital instrument in the game is the bearing compass, a circular card template with a hole punched through the middle. It is used for two purposes. Firstly, to check the fire arcs of your own ships to see which weapons they can bring to bear against the enemy. Doing so is simple: place the template over your ship so that the hole in the center is above the center of the flying stand and the two arrows are pointing along the length of the ship. This places the 90 degree quadrants so that one is in front, one is behind, and one is to each side of the ship.

Secondly, it is used to find out what aspect a target is presenting to a ship firing at it. In this case the bearing compass is placed over the target in the same way as described previously.

### Fire Arcs

Weapons have a limited field of fire depending on where they are mounted on the vessel. The different fire arcs are: front, left, right and rear. Some weapons can shoot in more than one fire arc. For example, most dorsal weaponry can be fired left, front, or right. Some weapons can even fire all round. Other weapons may have an area-of-effect that is not directed at a particular target, instead affecting everything around the firing vessel. If the line of fire is between two arcs, the attacking player gets to choose which arc to use for that phase. This applies to both shooting and target orientation.

*Standard naval terms are used frequently within this book and the main BFG sourcebooks. The meaning of each term is below.*

*Prow = front; Stern (aft) = rear; Port = left side; Starboard = right side; Dorsal = the top/spine of the hull (dorsal weapons usually fire left/front/right).*

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Bearing Compass" p.4; "Fire Arcs" (The Shooting Phase) p.16*

## 10. Movement

**Moving ships:** move your ships one at a time, in any order; each moves up to its speed before you choose the next. Ships never move simultaneously, even in a squadron.

**Move distance:** a ship moves up to its Speed (the Spd value on its profile). All Ahead Full increases this and Burn Retros reduces it (see Special Orders); damage and blast markers can also slow a ship.

**Minimum move:** a ship must move at least half its speed unless it is on Burn Retros. If it can't (due to damage, blast markers and so forth), it moves the maximum distance it can instead. A ship that moves less than 5cm, for any reason, counts as Defences on the Gunnery Table.

**Turning:** ships move straight ahead and may make one turn during their move, unless special orders say otherwise. A turn can be up to the ship's Turn value (45° or 90°). Before turning, a ship must move straight ahead: a battleship 15cm, a cruiser 10cm; an escort can turn at any point in its move. Only forward movement made in the current movement phase counts toward this distance.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Movement Phase - Basic Moves", p.13*

## 11. Shields & Blast Markers

Blast markers represent explosions, shockwaves, radiation clouds, debris, unexploded warheads and plasma bursts. They are placed where the event happens and stay in play until removed in a later end phase (section 36). Once placed they never move: they are part of the battlefield while in play.

### Shields

Each point of a ship's shield strength blocks one hit. Place a blast marker touching the ship's base for each hit blocked.

A ship's shield strength is reduced by 1 for each blast marker touching its base, however the marker came into contact, to a minimum of 0. At 0 its shields are down, and further hits damage the ship.

Shields are restored automatically as the ship leaves contact with blast markers. A ship may need to move away from several markers before any of its shields come back.

Shields stop direct fire: lances, macrobatteries and nova cannon. Boarding actions, ramming, torpedoes, attack craft and similar attacks bypass shields entirely.

*Clarification (Remastered p.69):* a ship whose shields are down only because of blast markers does not roll for damage when moving through blast markers. That roll is only for ships with no shields or permanently disabled shields (below).

### Placing Blast Markers

Blast markers are placed for each hit absorbed by shields, for destroyed ships (section 34) and for missed nova cannon shots (section 14). Other placements are given in the relevant rules.

**Shield impacts:** place the marker touching the target's base as close as possible to the origin of the attack, narrow end pointing away from the ship's peg. Markers from several impacts in the same turn do not overlap one another: fan them out around the base, as close as possible to the origin of the attack. Avoid overlapping other blast markers (from previous turns, around other ships or from other effects) unless there is no room, and avoid touching ships that are nearby but not in base contact unless there is no other way. If forced to choose, overlap other blast markers first. As a result, a small base can take 3-5 fanned markers in one turn and a large base 5-8, depending on spacing.

**Multiple ships under fire:** if the ship under fire has other ships in base contact, markers fanned around its base may overlap those ships' bases (following the restrictions above), affecting them normally in all respects.

*Clarifications (Remastered pp.68-69):* a blast marker affects only the ships whose bases it physically touches. A ship near the target but not touching it is never affected, and a ship that is touching cannot be treated as out of contact. Touching a ship that has a blast marker is not enough: the marker itself must touch the ship's base.

### Moving Through Blast Markers

If a vessel moves through any blast markers, it reduces its speed that phase by 5cm, regardless of the number of markers actually moved through. A vessel is considered to be moving through blast markers even if it is moving away from markers it is in contact with at the beginning of the movement phase. If a ship cannot elect to slow down (such as those on All Ahead Full special orders), and comes into contact with a blast marker within the last 5cm of its movement, it must instead stop no farther than the point it comes into contact with the marker (and not before).

If a ship is not equipped with shields, or its shields have been permanently disabled (rather than simply depleted) and it moves through at least one blast marker, roll a D6. On a result of 6, it suffers 1 point of damage. Likewise, when an ordnance marker moves through any blast markers, it is destroyed on a D6 roll of 6. These tests only need to be made once per move, no matter how many blast markers a unit encounters along its path.

### Shooting Through Blast Markers

If a ship's line of fire passes through any blast markers (including those touching a ship's base), it takes a right column shift on the Gunnery Table. Lances and nova cannon are unaffected. Blast markers take effect immediately, so they can affect later shooting in the same turn in which they are placed.

### Optional: Modern Blast Markers

XR offers these as optional 'modern' blast marker rules. They simplify firing through blasts, but can be more complex with more than a few ships on the table. Battlefleet Gothic Remastered uses this approach as its standard rules, with the further differences listed after them.

**Intervening blasts:** a ship in base contact with any blast markers always counts as behind them for enemy shooting and ordnance attacks, wherever the markers actually are. Enemy gunnery takes a right column shift, and attacking ordnance counts as moving through blast markers (destroyed on a D6 roll of 6).

**Overlapping bases:** a ship whose base overlaps the base of a ship in contact with blast markers also counts as in contact with them. This does not cascade along a chain of overlapping ships: if Ships 1 and 2 each overlap Ship 3 but not each other, and Ship 1 touches a blast marker, Ship 3 counts as in contact but Ship 2 does not. When bases are stacked or overlapping, there is no limit to the number of ships' shields one blast marker can take down (Remastered p.69).

**Remastered differences** (Remastered pp.68-69), used with the modern rules above:

*Whole-base contact:* a ship touching any blast marker counts as in contact all round its base and in every arc, for Leadership, movement, shooting, ordnance attacks, boarding and every other blast marker effect, including when it ends its move in contact for any reason.

*Firing ship in contact:* a ship takes the right column shift if its line of fire passes through blast markers, or if it or its target is in base contact with blast markers. Lances and nova cannon are still unaffected.

*Limit on spill-over:* one round of shooting from a ship or squadron may place blast markers in contact with at most one ship besides its target (two in total); the marker goes between the target and the ship closest to it in the line of fire. Exceptions: a ship with several shields (such as a battleship) that takes three or four markers whose normal fan touches more than one ship in actual base contact affects every ship the markers physically touch; and every ship whose base overlaps or is stacked on the target is affected, as above.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Shooting Phase - Shields, Blast Markers, Modern Blast Markers", pp.21-23; Battlefleet Gothic Remastered Rulebook, "Blast Markers", pp.68-69*

## 12. Ramming

A ship on All Ahead Full special orders may try to ram a single enemy vessel as it moves. It can never ram more than one enemy in a single move.

**Declaring:** declare the target after passing the command check for All Ahead Full, but before rolling for its speed bonus. Once declared, the ram cannot be abandoned or switched to another target, even if the ship ends its move in contact with several enemies or never reaches its target. Target priority does not apply: any enemy vessel may be nominated.

**Check course:** when the ram is declared, the ship takes a Leadership test: on 3D6 if the target is a smaller type of ship than the rammer, on 1D6 if it is a larger type, otherwise on 2D6. If it fails, the ship misses and completes its move normally without ramming (still on All Ahead Full).

**Movement:** the rammer still follows All Ahead Full: it cannot turn, and it must complete its full move if it survives the ram, even as a wrecked hulk. After resolving the ram it may still shoot, board, launch ordnance and so on, if it meets the usual requirements.

**Damage to the target:** the ram succeeds if any part of the rammer's base touches any part of the target's base during its move. Roll 1D6 for each point of the rammer's starting damage capacity. Each die that equals or beats the target's Armour on the facing struck inflicts 1 point of damage.

**Damage to the rammer:** if the ships meet front-to-front, or the target is a defence, the rammer rolls dice equal to the target's starting damage capacity; otherwise half that, rounding up. Each die that equals or beats the rammer's front Armour inflicts 1 point of damage.

Shields do not deflect ramming damage on either side. Damage is resolved immediately on contact: if either ship explodes, resolve hits from the explosion before the rammer finishes its move, and if the rammed ship explodes, its blast markers slow the rammer. Once the ram is resolved, the rammer continues its move (unless it exploded).

**Faction ramming rules:** Orks, *Hull Krusha* (one automatic hit on a successful ram; Xenos p.55) and the *Goffs* clan (always pass the Leadership test to ram; Xenos p.57). Space hulks count as defences for ramming (Chaos Space Hulk, Imperium p.44-45; Ork Space Hulk, Xenos p.69-70).

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Movement Phase - All Ahead Full: Ramming Speed!", p.37*

## 13. Direct Fire & Gunnery

Direct firing uses a ship's macrobatteries, lances and nova cannon. In order to make direct fire attacks, the firing ship must have at least some weapons within range and fire arc of the enemy.

**Firing order:** fire with one ship at a time, resolving all of its firing before choosing the next.

### Range

Measure the range from the firing ship to the target vessel. Any weapons which are out of range may not fire. Because ships vary immensely in size and shape, we use the stems of the models' bases as a pair of convenient center points for checking range.

### Fire Arcs

Weapons have a limited field of fire depending on where they are mounted on the vessel. The different fire arcs are: front, left, right and rear. Some weapons can shoot in more than one fire arc. If the line of fire is between two arcs, the attacking player gets to choose which arc to use for that phase. This applies to both shooting and target orientation.

### Target Priority

Normally, each weapon that a ship chooses to fire must target the nearest enemy within its firing arc. However, if the ship passes a leadership test (also called a target priority test), its weapons may be fired at any targets within range and firing arc. A vessel firing at enemy ships and defences may ignore enemy ordnance markers when determining the nearest enemy and vice versa for ships firing at enemy ordnance.

**Lances (direct firing):** If a lance weapon system is within range and fire arc of the target simply roll 1D6 per point of lance strength. Any dice which score a 4, 5 or 6 hit the target regardless of the target's Armor value and cause 1 damage point. Ships that pass a target priority test may split the strength of their lance weapons between multiple targets in a given fire arc. Naturally, a ship must have lance strength of 2 or greater (after any modifiers) in order to split fire this way.

**Weapons batteries (/Macrobatteries) (direct firing):** If a ship's macrobattery is within range and fire arc of the target, look up the battery's firepower on the ship's data sheet. Then look up the target's type and orientation on the Gunnery table. If a ship is firing multiple macrobatteries at the same target, total up the firepower of all the macrobatteries the firing ship is shooting at the target before consulting the Gunnery table. First find your total firepower on the column on the left of the table. Next look across the top of the table to find the target type you are shooting at.

Which way the target is traveling is important for gunnery purposes as it is much harder to hit a target moving across your sights (abeam) than one closing or moving away from you. By cross referencing the total firepower of the attack with the target type and orientation you will find out how many dice to roll to hit. Each dice roll which equals or beats the target's Armor value scores a hit and inflicts 1 point of damage.

**Mixing Different Gunnery Weapons:** If a ship or squadron has multiple distinct weapons that rely on the gunnery table (like a Space Marine strike cruiser), all of its gunnery weapons may be treated as firing simultaneously. In this case, resolve all rolls to-hit from each different gunnery weapon firing on the target first, then apply the results in the order the weapons were fired (each unique weapon type must still calculate its dice separately). Doing this prevents any column-shift penalties that would result from firing the weapons individually, but also commits all the chosen weapons to the attack, even if the first weapon ends up destroying the target.

**Firepower Greater Than 20:** If a combination of ships or weapons has a firepower value greater than 20, look up 20 and the remaining firepower separately and add the resulting numbers of dice together. For example, a squadron of two Carnages with a firepower 32 salvo against an abeam capital ship would roll 7+4 dice for its attack (firepower 20+12).

**Gunnery Modifiers:** Modifiers are applied in the form of column shifts. A good modifier means that you move across the Gunnery table one column to the left when you work out how many Hit dice to roll. A bad modifier means you move across one column to the right. No target aspect or modifier can adjust shooting beyond the far left or right columns on the gunnery table. The gunnery modifiers are summarized as follows:

- Target within 15cm: Left-shift.

- Target more than 30cm away: Right-shift.

- Firing through blast markers: Right-shift.

**Splitting Fire:** Ships that pass a target priority test may split the firepower of their gunnery weapons between multiple targets in a given fire arc. Naturally, a ship must have firepower of 2 or greater (after any modifiers, which must be applied before working out target orientation on the Gunnery table) in order to split fire this way.

**Gunnery Table:** see Appendix 7, p.171.

**Gunnery Table notes:** 'capital ships' on the table means cruisers and battleships. Defences and ordnance always use their own column (plus any column shifts), whatever their orientation, even when fired on by ships that normally count every target as one aspect.

### Stationary Targets

A ship that does not move at least 5cm, for any reason, counts as Defences on the Gunnery Table (see section 10, Movement).

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook (current), "The Shooting Phase - Direct Fire", p.16-18; minimum-movement / stationary rule from "Movement", p.13*

## 14. Nova Cannon

A nova cannon is a massive weapon, normally mounted in the prow of a ship so that the recoil it generates can be compensated for by the vessel's engines. It fires a projectile at incredible velocity, using graviometric impellers to accelerate it to close to light speed. The projectile implodes at a preset distance after firing, unleashing a force more potent than a dozen plasma bombs.

**Nova Cannon Rules:** To fire a nova cannon, place the template so that the entire outer edge is anywhere between 30cm and 150cm from the firing ship and within the correct firing arc (usually the front). It does not have to be centered on a single enemy vessel and can be placed so that it touches more than one ship. However, a target priority test must be made if the closest target more than 30cm away is not at least partially under the template, and the template must not touch any friendly ships.

When the template is placed, check the range. If the target is within 45cm, roll a scatter die and 1D6. Roll 2D6 if the range is between 45cm to 60cm, and 3D6 if the range is between 60cm and 150cm. If the scatter die rolls a "Hit," the template remains where it is placed. If it is a miss, the template moves the distance rolled (in cm) in the direction shown by the miss arrow. Any ships attempting to use Brace for Impact orders against a nova cannon must do so before this roll is made.

Any vessel whose base touches the template takes one automatic hit. Any vessel whose base touches the hole at the template's center takes D6 hits, regardless of its Armor value. Any ordnance touching the template is automatically removed. If no ships were hit by the nova cannon place a single blast marker under the center of the template.

Note that nova cannon are not affected in any way by Lock On and Reload Ordnance special orders. A vessel on any other special orders may not fire its nova cannon at all.

**Obstacles:** The nova cannon is a line of sight weapon and cannot fire through any obstacles or celestial phenomena that act as normal line of sight obstructions, such as planets, moons, asteroid fields, etc. However, such obstacles may be fired upon if desired. If a direct hit is scored on the scatter dice, place D6 blast markers in contact with the obstacle or field's edge.

**Template:** A nova cannon template is a circle with a 5cm outer diameter and a 1.2cm diameter hole in the center. In a pinch, the Games Workshop small blast template may be used for this purpose.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Shooting Phase - Nova Cannon", p.19*

## 15. Ordnance: Launching, Moving & Attacking

Ordnance (torpedoes, mines and attack craft) is represented by markers that move across the table in each ordnance phase. Markers interact with ships and other ordnance whenever they make contact, whatever the turn or phase.

**Launching:** ordnance is launched at the end of the shooting phase (place the marker on the ship's base) and moves and attacks in the ordnance phase. A ship that launches any ordnance must reload with Reload Ordnance before it can launch again, however many markers it actually placed: a carrier with four launch bays cannot place two markers and save the other two for later, and the same applies to torpedoes and mines.

**Ships with both attack craft and torpedoes or mines:** launching one type does not affect the other. If such a ship launches only one type, it may still launch the other later without reloading. For example, a Dictator that launches its attack craft but not its torpedoes, then fails its Reload Ordnance check the next turn, may still launch its torpedoes that turn.

**Staying loaded:** a ship's ordnance stays loaded until it is launched, however many turns pass and whatever special orders the ship receives. Being crippled or braced still affects the strength of all its ordnance as normal.

**Attack craft limit:** a fleet may never have more attack craft markers in play than the total number of launch bays remaining in the fleet. This limits only the number of markers, not which craft are used or which ships launch them. Ships with random-strength launch bays count their maximum possible value towards the limit.

**Recalling attack craft:** before launching attack craft, a player may remove friendly squadrons from play to free up launch capacity. If the fleet's capacity drops below the number of markers it has in play (for example through critical damage or lost ships), the owner must remove the excess the next time a new squadron is launched.

**Combining ordnance in squadrons:** members of the same squadron may combine their attack craft into larger waves, and their torpedoes into larger salvos. The combining ships must be in contiguous base contact (an unbroken chain of contact, as for coherency), and the combined marker's launch move is measured from the ship in the group furthest from its destination. Squadron members that do not contribute may launch individually or as a separate group, but each ship may contribute to only one combining group per turn. Torpedoes launched this way do not hit ships in the combining group, but must end their first move within the firing arc of every ship in the group; they can still hit squadron members that are outside base contact with the firing ship and not part of the group.

**Moving:** in the ordnance phase both players move and attack with all their ordnance, including ordnance launched on earlier turns. The player whose turn it is always moves all of theirs first. Each marker moves according to its speed and the turning rules for its type (sections 16-1922), and markers in a wave or salvo stay in contact as their rules require. Ordnance moving through blast markers is destroyed on a D6 roll of 6 (section 11).

**Ordnance attacks:** a marker that contacts a ship's base or an enemy ordnance marker attacks it immediately, with the effects given for its type. This includes contact made by a ship moving into ordnance during its own move. Unless stated otherwise, ordnance attacks ignore shields, and ordnance other than torpedoes is usually removed from play after attacking.

**First contact:** ordnance must attack the first valid target it contacts, but is not required to move into contact with the nearest enemy. If two ships overlap so that it is impossible to tell which is closer to the attacking ordnance, the attacker chooses which one is attacked. Attack craft have one exception (section 18).

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Ordnance Phase - Using Ordnance, Launching Ordnance, Moving Ordnance, Ordnance Attacks", p.26; "Advanced Rules - Shooting by Squadrons", p.45*

## 16. Torpedoes

The term 'torpedo' has always been used to describe any long-range missile carried by a spaceship. A typical anti-ship torpedo is over 200 feet long and powered by a plasma reactor, which also acts as a sizable portion of its warhead, turning it into a devastating plasma bomb. The area of a ship given over to the torpedo tubes is a massive space criss-crossed by lifts, hoists and gantry cranes for moving the huge missiles from the armored magazine silos where they are stored to the launch tubes.

Once a torpedo has been launched, the plasma drive propels it forward at high speed, whilst beginning an energy build-up that culminates in its detonation. Torpedoes have a limited ability to detect a target and will alter course to intercept if they pass within a few thousand kilometers of a vessel.

**Torpedo Rules:** Torpedoes are launched by ships with torpedo tubes, which are typically fitted to the prow of a ship. In-game, torpedo salvos have a strength value and a speed value, which are shown on a vessel's profile. The higher the strength, the more torpedoes there are in a salvo; the higher the speed, the faster they move across the battlefield. All torpedo salvos are represented by 2.5cm x 2cm markers. In older editions, this is equivalent to a 'Strength-3' torpedo token. Place dice next to each marker showing the actual strength of the salvo.

Torpedoes launch in a straight line from the firing ship's stem towards any point within the weapon's firing arc, moving a distance equal to their speed and continuing in this direction in each subsequent ordnance phase (unless specifically able to change heading) until they run out of strength or leave the battlefield. Torpedo salvos must always move their full speed, keeping the marker's center on the same point along the line of fire at all times.

If a torpedo marker contacts a ship's base (friend or foe) it attacks. Roll a D6 for every point of strength in the torpedo salvo. Each dice result which equals or beats the ship's Armor value scores one hit, applying the appropriate effect for the torpedo's type, as described in its special rules. The torpedo salvo continues moving after the attack but its strength is reduced by 1 for every hit it inflicted. Torpedoes that re-roll missed attacks must do so, even if the target is already destroyed.

On the turn it is launched, a torpedo salvo cannot attack a target that is not actually in its firing arc, no matter how close it is or whether the marker contacts the target's base. Also, torpedoes may never attack a single target more than once per full turn, even if they are already in contact with it at the start of the movement or ordnance phase. For example, a torpedo marker that ends its movement overlapping the base of a vessel will attack it upon contact, but will not attack that same ship again at the beginning of that ship's movement phase. Vessels in such a situation may assume the torpedoes have flown off behind them and move off of the torpedo marker.

**Premature Detonation:** A salvo of torpedoes can be triggered prematurely (removing it from play) by any of the following:

- On a D6 roll of 6 while moving through any blast markers.

- If the salvo is fired on by direct fire weapons and any hits are scored.

- If the salvo hits another torpedo marker.

### Splitting Torpedo Salvos

A single ship firing torpedoes at strength 7 or more may split its salvo into two separate markers. The two salvos must go in different directions (never both at the same target or in the same direction), and neither may contain fewer than three torpedoes. This applies only to individual ships; squadrons follow the combining rules in section 15.

### Firing Through Allies

Torpedoes normally attack any ship they contact, friend or foe. However, a ship may fire its torpedoes through a friendly ship without attacking it if that ship is in base contact with the firer when the torpedoes are launched. This cannot be used to combine torpedoes from ships in different squadrons, but squadron members in base contact benefit from it as normal.

### Turning Torpedoes

Torpedoes that can turn are rotated about the centre of the marker at the start of their move, so that no edge moves further than the salvo's speed (the inside edge may move less). A salvo cannot turn in the ordnance phase of the turn it is launched, so turning torpedoes may only be launched into the firing arc on their profile, like any other.

### Torpedo Types

**Standard torpedoes:** used by any ship whose torpedo type is not specified. They cannot turn once launched, and each hit inflicts 1 point of damage.

**Boarding torpedoes:** manned, so they may turn up to 45° before moving. They always ignore friendly ships and attack craft, and may ignore drifting or burning hulks; otherwise they must attack the first enemy vessel they contact, and are still destroyed by contact with another torpedo marker. Each hit makes an immediate hit-and-run raid on the target (section 31).

**Short burn torpedoes:** speed 40cm. After the salvo has moved and made any attacks, roll a D6: on a 6 it runs out of fuel and is replaced with a blast marker. Otherwise they are standard torpedoes.

**Barrage bombs:** fired normally, and may also be launched while the ship is in low orbit, where they move like standard torpedoes (they are unaffected by gravity). Each salvo that strikes a planet in a planetary assault scenario earns +1 assault point at strength 6 or less, or +2 at strength 7 or more. Each hit on an enemy ship inflicts 1 point of damage that does not ignore shields.

**Guided torpedoes:** may turn up to 45° before moving, but only if the launching ship first passes a Leadership test. If it fails, one enemy ship may try to take control by passing a Leadership test of its own; if it succeeds, its owner turns the torpedoes instead. Each hit inflicts 1 point of damage.

**Seeking torpedoes:** one salvo per game. In each ordnance phase after launch, the salvo must turn up to 45° before moving, to point as close as possible towards the nearest enemy ship (the largest, if several are equally near). They ignore friendly ships and attack craft. Moving through blast markers, they detonate on a D6 roll of 5+ instead of 6. Each hit inflicts 1 point of damage.

**Melta torpedoes:** one salvo per game. Each hit inflicts no damage points but causes an automatic Fire critical hit, even on ships whose critical hit table has no Fire result. A ship carrying unused melta torpedoes that suffers a critical hit to its torpedo launcher location takes an extra D3 Fire critical hits.

**Vortex torpedoes:** one salvo per game. Each hit causes 1 point of damage and an automatic critical hit. A ship carrying unused vortex torpedoes that suffers a critical hit to its torpedo launcher location takes D3 extra damage and an automatic critical hit.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook (current), "The Ordnance Phase - Types of Ordnance", p.27; "Torpedo Special Rules", pp.29-30*

## 17. Mines

Space-borne mines almost always follow similar designs, regardless of which race constructs them. Although little more than self-guided explosives, mines are quite dangerous to enemy warships, and pose a substantial threat in large numbers.

**Space Mine Rules:** Each mine launcher on a vessel may launch one mine per turn (the number of mine launchers a ship has is often called its mine launcher strength). Mines are represented by 20mm square markers, like attack craft, but may never be grouped into waves or salvos. Once launched, a mine activates and will continue to function until it is destroyed. Mines must move exactly 10cm directly towards the nearest enemy vessel in every ordnance phase. If a mine contacts an enemy vessel it attacks it immediately, rolling 8D6 against the target's Armor value to hit. **Like other ordnance, mines ignore shields.** If a ship with mine launchers suffers critical damage to the launchers' location, it suffers an extra D3 damage as some mines explode!

**Note:** some printings of the rulebook read that damage from mines may be blocked by shields. This is an error: mines ignore shields, as above.

**Do not confuse this with minefields.** A *minefield* (p.67) is *treated as an asteroid field*, and failing the Leadership test to navigate one inflicts **asteroid-impact damage, against which shields DO apply normally** (see Asteroid Fields, p.24). A ship inside a minefield can suffer both: asteroid-impact damage (shields apply) **and**, on a 5+ detection, a mine attack (shields ignored). Two separate damage sources; they stack.

Mines follow all the regular rules for ordnance regarding moving through blast markers, shooting, and so forth. Except for fighters (which will remove mines just like any other ordnance), mines are not affected by, and do not affect, any other ordnance markers on the battlefield.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Ordnance Phase - Types of Ordnance", p.27*

## 18. Attack Craft

Attack craft are launched from a ship's launch bays: each bay may launch one squadron per turn, so a Dictator with four bays launches four squadrons. Each squadron is a single 20mm square marker, with a different icon for each type of craft. The owner chooses which craft to launch at the time, from those the carrier has, and may launch several types at once or only one.

**Movement:** attack craft may turn as often as desired and move in any direction, up to the speed on the carrier's profile. They need not move towards the nearest target or obstruction unless their move unavoidably brings them into contact. Attack craft that contact enemy ships or ordnance must attack immediately (sections 19-1922).

**Ships hiding under large bases:** unlike torpedoes and mines, attack craft may choose to attack an enemy ship with a small base 'hiding' within the footprint of a large ship's base, if they can actually reach it. This is the only way attack craft may ignore the first enemy ship they contact.

Resilient and multi-role attack craft, waves and combat air patrol: sections 23-2326.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Ordnance Phase - Attack Craft", p.28*

## 19. Fighters

Fighters are small, fast and extremely agile. They are only armed with weapons suitable for destroying torpedoes, mines, and other attack craft. In combat, the fighter's job is to intercept enemy ordnance and protect the vulnerable bombers and assault craft on their way to and from their target.

**Fighter Rules:** Fighter attacks have the following effects when they come into contact:

Vs Ordnance Markers. The defenders are scattered or destroyed during the dogfighting. The victorious fighters return to their mother ship for rearming and refueling. Remove both the defending and attacking markers from play. Note that this is a compulsory attack; fighters must always intercept enemy attack craft and mines they encounter, as well as any torpedoes (friend or foe) that they contact.

Vs Ships. The puny fighters cannot hope to damage the much larger vessel, so they will simply hold position out of harm's way. Leave the squadron marker in play (do not roll any turret attacks). Fighters in base contact with friendly ships may move with them to screen against enemy ordnance in a process called combat air patrol, which is explained further in the Advanced Rules section.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Attack Craft Special Rules", p.31*

## 20. Bombers

Bombers are slower, heavier craft with destructive anti-ship weapons. Though vulnerable to enemy fighters, bombers can be a serious threat to ships.

**Bomber Rules:** Bomber attacks have the following effects when they move into contact:

Vs Ordnance Markers. Enemy fighters will remove bombers normally, as described in their rules. Any other ordnance will simply be avoided - leave both markers in play (no attacks are resolved).

Vs Ships. The bombers make an attack run on the ship. Make D6 rolls to hit against the ship's lowest Armor value for each attacking bomber squadron. The number of attacks the squadron makes reduces by one for each turret on the ship (minimum zero). Note that this does not include any bonuses from vessels massing turrets, but still applies even if the defending ship fired its turrets at torpedoes or mines in the same phase. Remove the squadron markers from play once the attack has been completed.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Attack Craft Special Rules", p.31*

## 21. Torpedo Bombers

Torpedo bombers are bombers reconfigured to carry small anti-ship torpedoes, so they can attack from further away.

Against other ordnance, torpedo bombers count as bombers. They cannot attack ships directly. Instead, at the start of any ordnance phase after the turn they are launched, a torpedo bomber squadron may be replaced by a strength-2 torpedo salvo facing in any direction. The salvo has limited fuel and is removed at the end of the phase in which it was fired. A wave of torpedo bombers may combine their salvos, like a squadron of ships in base contact.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Attack Craft Special Rules - Torpedo Bombers", p.31*

## 22. Assault Boats

Assault boats clamp onto a target and breach its hull, so that boarding parties can sabotage the ship from inside.

**Vs ordnance markers:** enemy fighters remove assault boats normally. Any other ordnance is avoided: leave both markers in play, with no attack.

**Vs ships:** each assault boat squadron makes an immediate hit-and-run raid against the ship (section 31), then is removed from play.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Attack Craft Special Rules - Assault Boats", p.31*

## 23. Resilient Attack Craft

Some attack craft are so large and heavily armored that interception by enemy fighters may not always guarantee victory. Likewise, some fighters have expanded fuel reserves and armaments, allowing them to operate away from their mother ship for extended periods of time. This is represented by a 'resilient' save noted in an attack craft's description.

Attack craft that are resilient have a save (usually 4+) against other ordnance. When a squadron of resilient attack craft intercept or are intercepted by another ordnance marker, roll a D6 - if the result is equal or better than the squadron's save value, it remains in play rather than being removed.

Resilient attack craft can only attempt this save once per phase, regardless of whether they are attacking or being attacked. Furthermore, if they pass their save and remain in play, they must immediately stop where the interaction took place and cannot move any further in the same phase (although fighters on combat air patrol will still move with their parent ship). In all cases, resilient attack craft that fail to make their save are immediately removed.

Resilient saves do not work against blast markers, turrets, enemy shooting attacks, or after attacking an enemy ship. In these cases, a squadron cannot use its resilient save to avoid being removed. Resilient multi-role attack craft that can behave as fighters must do so when in contact with ordnance, but any surviving markers may attack enemy ships in base contact after resolving other ordnance interactions.

**Fighters and Multiple Attacks:** If a resilient fighter squadron is still in contact with enemy ordnance markers when it passes its save, it will automatically intercept again and subsequently be removed as normal, since it cannot use its save twice in the same phase. See the following example:

A wave of 2 resilient Space Marine Thunderhawks (which can behave as fighters) moves into contact with a wave of 2 Chaos Swiftdeath fighters:

The lead squadrons interact first, intercepting each other. The Thunderhawk squadron passes its save, remaining the lead squadron of its wave, while the lead Swiftdeath squadron is removed normally.

The two waves are still in contact, so their lead markers interact again (the first Thunderhawk and the second Swiftdeath), removing both squadrons this time as the Thunderhawk cannot use its save again that phase, leaving the second Thunderhawk marker alone as the only survivor.

Note that the resolution process is the same even if both squadrons are resilient. That is, if a squadron of resilient fighters intercepts a squadron of resilient attack craft and both squadrons pass their saves, the two markers must continue to interact and thus both will be removed as normal.

**Splitting Resilient Waves:** If a wave containing resilient attack craft intercepts or is intercepted by ordnance before the wave has moved, any markers which use their resilient save (and must stop moving) may be split from the wave (following the rules on page 32) after resolving the interaction, thus allowing the other squadrons in the wave to move as normal. If they are not split off, the wave cannot move.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook (current), "Advanced Rules - Ordnance Phase", p.39*

## 24. Multi-Role Attack Craft

Certain fleets throughout the 41st Millennium make use of attack craft that are not restricted to just one type of mission profile, instead employing versatile multi-role attack craft.

Multi-role attack craft combine the abilities of two or more types of standard attack craft. In general, they are subject to all of the normal rules for each of their types, with the following clarifications:

**Multi-role Fighters:** Since fighters are required to intercept all torpedoes, enemy attack craft, and hostile mines they contact, multi-role squadrons that can behave as fighters are also subject to this restriction. This means that they cannot choose to act as some other type of attack craft in order to avoid interacting or being removed.

**Bombers & Assault Boats:** Likewise, multi-role squadrons that can function as bombers or assault boats are required to attack any enemy ships they contact; they cannot choose to act as fighters or torpedo bombers to avoid making an attack run.

When attacking in a wave, all multi-role attack craft that can function as both bombers and assault boats must attack in the same way - either as bombers or as assault boats, not a mix of the two. Choose after the defending ship fires its turrets.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook (current), "Advanced Rules - Ordnance Phase", p.39*

## 25. Waves of Attack Craft

When a ship launches its attack craft, it may choose to combine any number of them into a wave, which will move and attack together. To show attack craft deployed as a wave, place the markers so they're in one of the two formations below:

- Close: A wave of attack craft in close formation are represented by a vertical stack of markers arranged with the lead marker on top and the rest in order below it. Fighters must be placed ahead of other attack craft (i.e. as close to the lead as possible).

- Loose: A wave in loose formation are arranged as a block of adjacent markers with the smallest perimeter possible (such as 3 x 2). In this case, the wave is ordered left-to-right, front-to-back, starting with the front-left marker. As above, fighters must be placed ahead of other attack craft.

The order of the markers (and which one is the lead marker) is important for resolving interactions with ships and other ordnance. For this purpose, any multi-role attack craft that function as fighters must be placed between pure fighters and other craft.

**Forming and splitting:** a wave cannot change formation once launched, and formations cannot be mixed. Squadrons may split off from a wave when it moves, alone or as a new wave in the same formation. Once separated, attack craft can never combine or recombine into a wave.

**Open information:** the contents of every wave are open: a close-formation stack cannot hide or bluff what is in it.

**Wave movement:** a wave moves as a single marker for all movement, including contact with ships or ordnance and moving through blast markers (one roll for the whole wave). It moves at the speed of its slowest squadron.

**Shooting at waves:** a wave counts as a single marker for enemy shooting, and is destroyed entirely by a single hit.

**Waves against ordnance:** squadrons in a wave interact one at a time, starting with the lead marker and continuing in order, until one side is entirely removed or the lead squadron does not interact with the opposing marker. Keep the wave in contact as squadrons are removed.

**Waves against ships:** when a wave contacts a ship that any of its squadrons must attack, the ship rolls its turrets once against the whole wave. Each success destroys one squadron, starting with the lead marker and continuing in order. The remaining squadrons then attack together. Afterwards the whole wave is removed, including squadrons that did not attack.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Ordnance Phase - Waves of Attack Craft", p.32*

## 26. Combat Air Patrol (CAP)

Due to the danger posed by enemy bombers, assault boats, and torpedoes, fleets will often employ fighter escorts to supplement the turret defences of their light and medium cruisers (although particularly important battleships will sometimes receive a few squadrons as well). This practice is called combat air patrol, or CAP.

**Joining / Leaving CAP:** Any fighters or attack craft that behave as fighters may, at any point during their normal movement, be placed on CAP for a friendly ship in base contact. Squadrons on CAP move with the parent vessel during its movement phase, remaining in contact rather than moving independently in their ordnance phase (in other words, no double moves).

A fighter squadron which moves further than its normal speed as a result of escorting a faster ship, such as a Thunderhawk (Speed: 20cm) escorting a Cobra (Speed: 30cm), also forfeits its move in the opposing player's ordnance phase, though it is still capable of defending the ship they are currently escorting.

A fighter squadron will usually stay on CAP until it is removed by ordnance or its parent vessel is destroyed. However, it may voluntarily leave CAP at the start of the owning player's movement phase or during the owning player's part of the enemy ordnance phase. In both cases, it will immediately return to the regular rules for moving and attacking.

**CAP Special Rules:** Fighters on CAP will always intercept any enemy ordnance the escorted ship encounters, regardless of where the ordnance actually makes contact. This is the only instance where ordnance may interact without actually being in physical contact. In all other respects they will intercept enemies normally.

**Multiple Fighters on CAP:** More than one fighter squadron on CAP for a single ship function as independent markers in all respects and are never a wave, even if markers were part of one when placed on CAP. (Any non-fighters that are part of a wave when the fighters are placed on CAP will automatically split off).

As a result, if the ship encounters blast markers or is subsequently destroyed, the fighter squadrons must roll separately against any effects. Any fighters that survive the destruction of their parent vessel will thereafter act as independent ordnance markers and may move again in the subsequent ordnance phase.

**Multi-role / Other Attack Craft:** Other attack craft which do not behave as fighters cannot be placed on a ship's CAP for any reason. For example, a bomber squadron cannot be placed on CAP in order to attack enemy ships that the escorted vessel encounters during its movement.

However, multi-role ordnance markers that can act as fighters may still be placed on CAP normally. In this case, such squadrons are capable of attacking enemy ships they encounter, but may only do so during the ordnance phase or if an enemy vessel moves into contact during the enemy's movement phase. They may not otherwise attack a ship unless they leave CAP first. See the examples below:

An Ork Terror Ship with two Ork fighta-bommas on CAP decides to ram an Imperial cruiser. Despite contacting the enemy, the fighta-bommas dragged along with the Terror Ship do not automatically attack the Imperial cruiser. Instead, they must wait until the ordnance phase and may only attack if the escorted ship actually ended its movement in base contact with the target vessel.

On the other hand, if the Imperial cruiser moves into contact with the Terror Ship for any reason, the fighta-bommas will attack it immediately (if the contact is from ramming, the cruiser's ram attacks are resolved first, however).

Note that because the markers on CAP are not in a wave, if an enemy ship is destroyed by multi-role attack craft on CAP, only remove the markers that actually attacked.

CAP vs. Friendly Ordnance

It is possible (though difficult) to defend friendly attack craft by moving them within the perimeter of a ship's base that has fighters on CAP, since enemy ordnance which contacts the escorted ship will be intercepted by the fighters on CAP first.

Furthermore, fighters on CAP will not intercept torpedoes or mines while they are being launched from the parent ship or from friendly ships in base contact, including massed torpedo salvos launched by a squadron. However, they will defend against torpedoes and enemy mines in any other instance.

Resilient Attack Craft vs. CAP

Resilient bombers and assault boats that end their movement in base contact with a ship escorted by CAP may attack it normally, even if stopped after using their save (provided that they do not need to move any further and that they survive the vessel's turret attacks).

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Advanced Rules - Ordnance Phase", p.40*

## 27. Turret Suppression

Waves containing both fighters and bombers gain an additional bonus, as the fighters may temporarily neutralize enemy turrets before the bombers make their attack run. This is resolved as follows:

For each surviving fighter in the wave, you may treat one squadron of bombers as making exactly 3 attacks (instead of rolling randomly), regardless of the target's Turrets value. Bombers able to re-roll their number of attacks add +1 extra attack in this case (for a total of 4 instead of 3).

**For example:** A wave of 3 fighters and 3 bombers attacks a ship with 4 turrets, losing two squadrons before attacking. This removes the two front-most fighter markers, leaving the wave with 1 fighter and 3 bombers. One bomber may then make 3 attacks, while the other two make D6-4 each.

Multi-role squadrons that function as both fighters and bombers may either suppress turrets or perform attacks (choose before the defender fires its turrets). At least one squadron must attack the ship however, and all the squadrons suppressing turrets must be as close to the front of the wave as possible (meaning they will be the first to be removed by turret fire).

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook (current), "The Ordnance Phase - Launching Waves of Attack Craft", p.32*

## 28. Defence Turrets

Most warships mount numerous point-defence systems and automatic turrets for shooting down enemy ordnance during their final attack run. A ship's main armament is simply too huge and slow to track ordnance at such close ranges. However, turrets may be fired immediately whenever enemy ordnance touches the ship's base. This always takes effect before resolving any ordnance attacks. To fire turrets, roll a D6 for each point of the ship's Turrets value, and apply the relevant effect below:

**Vs Torpedoes:** Each die that scores a 4, 5, or 6 reduces the salvo's strength by 1.

**Vs Mines:** If at least one die scores a 4, 5, or 6, the attacking marker rolls 4D6 to hit instead of 8D6.

**Vs Attack Craft:** Each die that scores a 4, 5, or 6 removes 1 attacking marker. While this makes ships nearly impervious to individual squadrons, attack craft usually come in waves (as described on page 32).

Due to the greater difficulty tracking attack craft as opposed to torpedoes and/or mines, a ship that uses its turrets to defend against attack craft may not use them against any other type of ordnance in the same phase, and vice versa. Otherwise, a ship may always fire its turrets when attacked by ordnance.

**Massed Turrets:** Ships in base contact with a vessel under attack by ordnance may 'mass' their turrets with it. For each ship contributing its turrets, add +1 to the turret strength of the defending ship (limit +3). Ships that mass turrets are subject to the same restrictions as the defending ship regarding attack craft and other ordnance. Ships that are crippled or have no turrets may not contribute to this bonus, but may benefit from allied ships' turrets normally. As ships move individually, it is not usually possible to mass turrets during a vessel's move unless it contacts ordnance while moving through an allied ship's base.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook (current), "The Ordnance Phase - Defence Turrets", p.28*

## 29. Massed Turrets

Ships in base contact with a vessel under attack by ordnance may 'mass' their turrets with it. For each ship contributing its turrets, add +1 to the turret strength of the defending ship (limit +3). Ships that mass turrets are subject to the same restrictions as the defending ship regarding attack craft and other ordnance. Ships that are crippled or have no turrets may not contribute to this bonus, but may benefit from allied ships' turrets normally. As ships move individually, it is not usually possible to mass turrets during a vessel's move unless it contacts ordnance while moving through an allied ship's base.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Ordnance Phase - Defence Turrets / Massed Turrets", p.28*

## 30. Shooting at Ordnance

Ordnance may be fired at in the shooting phase with direct fire weapons. Ordnance is subject to the normal rules for target priority, but remember that ships firing at ordnance only count other enemy ordnance markers for the purposes of determining the nearest enemy.

Unless otherwise specified, rolls to-hit from direct fire weapons will only hit ordnance on a score of 6+, because they are such difficult targets. If an ordnance marker or wave is hit by enemy shooting it is removed from play.

Note that gunnery weapons firing at ordnance use the 'Ordnance' column on the Gunnery table. This is because ordnance targets are relatively small and fast moving. Target aspects are not taken into account, but column shifts for range, blast markers or other special rules do apply.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Ordnance Phase - Shooting at Ordnance", p.26*

## 31. Hit-and-Run Raids

A hit-and-run raid is a small boarding party sent to sabotage an enemy ship. Raids come from boarding torps (one raid per hit), assault boats (one raid per squadron that reaches the target) and teleport attacks.

**Order of play:**

> 1\. Boarding torps and assault boats must first survive the target's turrets.
>
> 2\. The target may Brace for Impact before the raids are rolled.
>
> 3\. Roll each raid (below).
>
> 4\. A braced target saves against each successful raid on a 4+.

**Vs. capital ships** (and other targets that can suffer critical damage): roll a single D6, not 2D6, and immediately apply that result from the Critical Hits table (Appendix 5). A final result of 1 or less has no effect. Modifiers apply first, so a raid with a +1 bonus (e.g. Space Marines) always causes damage.

**Vs. escorts and small targets** (that don't use the Critical Hits table): 1 point of damage on a 4+, applying modifiers as normal. This damage cannot cause a critical hit.

**Bonuses:** any bonus to a ship's hit-and-run raids (faction, refit, upgrade or crew) also applies to ordnance it launches, unless stated otherwise.

**Teleport attacks:** in its end phase, a ship may make one teleport attack against a single enemy ship within 10cm, resolved as a hit-and-run raid. The target's shields must be down, and it may not have more remaining hits than the attacker. Crippled ships, ships on special orders other than Lock On or Reload Ordnance, and escorts and defences with fewer than 3 starting hits cannot teleport. Teleport attacks are resolved before damage control and blast marker removal; if both players have teleport attacks or boarding actions to resolve, the player whose turn it is chooses the order.

**Faction teleport rules:** Space Marines, *Teleport Assault* (Imperium p.134); Inquisition & Grey Knights, Ordo honour guards (Imperium p.100); Chaos, *Champions of the Dark Gods* and *Chaos Terminators* (Imperium p.20); Orks, *Soopa Tellyporta* (Xenos p.56); Ramilies Star Fort and Fortress-Monastery quadrants (Imperium p.87, p.146). Tau and Nicassar vessels cannot teleport (Xenos p.78, p.95).

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Attack Craft Special Rules - Assault Boats", p.31; "Hit-and-Run Attacks", p.33; "Teleport Attacks", p.42*

## 32. Boarding Actions

**Moving in:** move the attacking vessel into base contact with the target and declare the boarding action. A ship that boards may not fire its weapons or launch ordnance in the same turn.

**Troop rating:** equal to the ship's remaining damage points. The defending ship also adds its turrets (after any other modifiers, and not counting massed turrets). The side with the higher troop rating gains a bonus to its boarding roll; apply only the highest:

| Your troop rating vs. the enemy's | Bonus |
|---|---|
| Higher | +1 |
| Double or greater | +2 |
| Triple or greater | +3 |
| Quadruple or greater | +4 |

**Crew damage bonus:** some factions add a crew damage bonus to their boarding roll (see below). Only the largest applicable bonus applies, unless stated otherwise.

**Resolving:** all boarding actions are resolved in the attacking player's end phase, before damage control and blast marker removal. The target must still be in base contact; the attacker cannot board a different enemy in contact instead. Both players roll a D6 and add:

- Enemy ship has blast markers in contact: +1

- Enemy ship is on special orders: +1

- Enemy ship is crippled: +2

- Higher troop rating: +1 to +4

- Crew damage bonus: varies

**Results:** the higher total wins. The loser suffers damage equal to the difference in scores; shields do not absorb it and Brace for Impact does not save it. Boarding damage does not cause critical hits in the usual way. Instead, find the difference on the table below, then roll a D6 for each ship involved (winner and loser) against its requirement; on a success that ship suffers a critical hit, resolved as normal.

| Difference | Result | Critical hit on (winner / loser) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stalemate | 5+ / 5+ |
| 2 | Heavy Fighting | 5+ / 4+ |
| 3 | Driven Back | 6+ / 3+ |
| 4 | Stormed | 6+ / 2+ |
| 5+ | Overwhelmed | None / Auto |

**Reduced to 0 by boarding:** do not roll for catastrophic damage; the ship becomes a drifting hulk. Excess damage still counts towards the result, and the critical hit roll is still made. A ship reduced to 0 by the critical damage itself rolls on the Catastrophic Damage table as normal. Ships that survive may move off normally in their next movement phase.

**Drawn combats:** if the totals are equal, the ships grapple and fight again in each end phase (between the original participants) until one side is destroyed. While locked together they may not move, shoot, launch ordnance or disengage, and do not add turrets to their troop rating; otherwise they act normally (defending against ordnance, using special orders, making teleport attacks, etc.). Once the combat is resolved, surviving ships may move off in their next turn.

**Several attackers:** several ships may combine to board one enemy, making a single roll and adding their troop ratings together. The defender gains each bonus that applies to any of the attackers (crippled, on special orders, etc.), even if the others do not qualify. If the attacking group loses, its owner divides the damage among the attacking ships, and the critical hit roll applies to each ship involved. Attackers may instead board separately, resolving each fight on its own.

**Escorts:** escorts may board like any other ship. Critical hits against escorts always inflict 1 point of damage instead of rolling on the table, so an escort can win a boarding action and still be destroyed.

**Faction boarding rules:** crew damage bonuses: Space Marines +2 (Imperium p.132), Chaos +1 (Imperium p.19), Orks +1 (Xenos p.54), Inquisition Battle Sister detachments (Imperium p.100). Troop rating: Chaos *Mark of Khorne* doubles it (Imperium p.19); an Ork *Warboss* doubles it (Xenos p.56) and *Goffs* add turrets when attacking (Xenos p.57); Tau Kor'vattra and Nicassar vessels halve it (Xenos p.78, p.95), and Nicassar never initiate boarding. Re-rolls: attackers re-roll against Adeptus Mechanicus ships (Imperium p.4); an *Inquisitorial retinue* forces the attacker to re-roll (Imperium p.99). Chaos Space Marine crews use the Space Marine rules (*Champions of the Dark Gods*, Imperium p.20). Ork *Grabbin' Klaws* (Xenos p.55). Individual ships and commanders with their own boarding rules (e.g. Lord Admiral Rath, Abaddon, the Slaverer, star forts) are covered in their entries.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "End Phase - Boarding Actions", pp.41-42*

## 33. Damage & Critical Hits

### Taking Hits

When a ship is damaged, note the number of hits it has taken on your fleet roster. Once a ship has lost half its damage points (rounding up) it is crippled. When a ship has lost all its hits, it is out of action and a roll needs to be made on the Catastrophic Damage table to see if it explodes in a spectacular fashion or simply drifts helplessly.

**Crippled Ships:** A ship which loses half its damage points is crippled. Crippled ships halve their lance strength, ordnance strength, gunnery firepower, turrets, and shields values (rounding up) and reduce their speed by 5cm. Crippled vessels may not fire their nova cannon, if they have one. When a crippled ship is on Brace For Impact special orders, the firepower of its gunnery weapons and strength of its lances and ordnance are halved again.

For example, a standard Lunar class cruiser has 8 hits and is therefore crippled when it has suffered 4 points of damage.

### Critical Hits

The weapons used in space are so destructive that when a ship is hit there is a chance that a vital location will be critically damaged. Critical hits can temporarily knock out a ship's weapons and engines, start fires or even breach its hull.

Roll a D6 for each hit scored on a ship (but not against its shields). On a roll of 1-5 the hit causes no extra effects. On a roll of 6 the hit causes critical damage. Roll 2D6 on the table below to see what effect it has. Do not roll for further critical hits for any damage caused by a critical hit. Note that a check for critical damage must be made for every point of damage scored on a ship, regardless of its source (unless otherwise specified).

**Critical Hits on Small Targets:** Escorts and defences with less than 3 starting hits suffer one point of damage whenever they suffer a critical hit, instead of rolling on the table.

**Repeated and impossible critical hits:** if a location suffers several repairable critical hits, all of them must be repaired before it works again, and their effects are not cumulative unless stated. If a critical hit cannot be applied (a ship with no prow weapons rolls Prow Armament Damaged, say), or a location that cannot be repaired is hit again, apply the next higher result instead (Engine Room Damaged, in that example).

**Critical Hits Table:** see Appendix 5, p.169.

## 34. Catastrophic Damage

A ship with no hits left is effectively out of action. There may still be some crew left alive, trapped in compartments that have yet to be breached, and there may still be power available in some sections of the ship, but the vessel can no longer fight. When a ship is reduced to zero damage, do the following based on their type:

**Capital Ships:** For mighty capital ships, there is a chance that the vessel will spectacularly explode as its reactors overload or its warp-drive malfunctions. Roll 2D6 on the Catastrophic Damage table below to see what happens.

**Escorts:** When escort ships are reduced to zero damage remove them from play and replace them with a blast marker, to represent the expanding cloud of debris left by their passing. Note that because a normal blast marker token is smaller than the diameter of a 32mm flying base, this blast marker cannot take down the shields of any ships in base contact unless they are actually overlapping the destroyed escort's base.

**Defences:** Any defences with 3 or more hits are treated as capital ships and may explode as normal. All other defences are replaced by a blast marker, like escorts.

**Exploding vessels:** explosions from the Catastrophic Damage table are area-of-effect attacks, not blocked by line-of-sight obstructions such as hulks, minefields or celestial phenomena. Place one blast marker exactly where the ship was, then fan the rest out around it and touching it, without overlapping other blast markers unless there is no room. The normal restriction against placing blast markers in contact with nearby ships does not apply.

**Moving drifting and blazing hulks:** the owner may move their hulks in any order. Hulks are affected normally by blast markers and other speed modifiers, and take 1 point of damage from moving through blast markers on a D6 roll of 6, like other ships without shields. This does not include the blast marker a hulk places at the end of its own move.

**Hulks in gravity wells:** a hulk in a gravity well must make any turns it gets towards the centre of the well, always turning the full amount, and makes both turns if it starts and ends its move in the well. A hulk that touches the base of a space hulk generating the well is removed (a blazing hulk first rolls once more on the Catastrophic Damage table).

**Hulks in combat:** hulks block lines of fire to ships behind their base, and have no shields, turrets or other defences (such as holofields or reactive hulls). A hulk that is the closest enemy may be ignored for target priority; firing at a hulk that is not the closest still needs a target priority test. Ships and ordnance may never fire on, board or deliberately launch torpedoes at their own side's hulks, but enemies may. Roll to hit against the destroyed ship's Armour. A hulk that takes any hits rolls once on the Catastrophic Damage table (replacing its current result): once per attacking ship, squadron or ordnance wave, not once per hit.

**Catastrophic Damage Table:** see Appendix 5, p.169.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Shooting Phase - Catastrophic Damage", pp.23-24*

## 35. Crippled Ships

A ship which loses half its damage points (rounding up) is crippled.

Crippled ships halve their lance strength, ordnance strength, gunnery firepower, turrets, and shields values (rounding up) and reduce their speed by 5cm. Crippled vessels may not fire their nova cannon, if they have one. When a crippled ship is on Brace For Impact special orders, the effectiveness of its armaments is halved again.

For example, a standard Lunar class cruiser has 8 hits and is therefore crippled when it has suffered 4 points of damage.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The Shooting Phase - Damage / Crippled Ships", p.20*

## 36. Damage Control & Blast Removal

**End phase order:** resolve boarding actions and teleport attacks first, then damage control, then blast marker removal.

Both players can attempt to repair critical damage during the end phase. Repair crews will be working continuously of course, welding up hull breaches, re-routing power conduits, putting out fires and generally trying to keep the ship functioning.

In each player's end phase, every capital ship with repairable critical damage rolls 1D6 per hit point it has remaining. Each result of '6' enables a ship to repair one critically damaged system. However, due to interference from debris and shield impacts, ships in contact with blast markers only roll half as many dice (rounding up). If both players need to make repair rolls, the player whose turn it is rolls for his or her ships first.

### Blast Marker Removal

With time, the debris and shock waves represented by blast markers will dissipate sufficiently to be of no further impediment to ships or their weapons. To represent this, the player whose turn it is rolls a D6 and removes that many blast markers from the table. Blast markers in contact with ships' bases may not be removed, but otherwise the player is free to choose which ones will go.

In addition, the player must also roll a D6 for each stationary defence (if any) and remove that many blast markers from its base. This only applies to immobile defences, not those whose movement has simply been reduced to 0cm.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "The End Phase - Damage Control / Blast Marker Removal", p.35*

## 37. Disengaging

Sometimes it is better to run away and live to fight another day. A ship may be crippled with no hope of defeating its attackers, or just outnumbered so heavily that its captain decides that it's hopeless to fight on. The classic approach to disengaging is to make a sudden course alteration and then cut all power so that the ship cannot be detected by its energy emissions. With luck the vessel will simply drift out of the immediate battle area and can set course for home once it is clear of the fighting. This strategy is far from guaranteed to succeed however, and may leave the ship vulnerable if it fails.

A ship may disengage from battle in one of two ways: first, any vessel that moves off of the table edge during play for any reason counts as being disengaged (whether it intended to or not)! Second, a ship or escort squadron may take a leadership test at the end of its movement phase with the following modifiers:

- Each blast marker within 5cm: +1 Ld

- Celestial phenomena within 15cm\*: +3 Ld

- Each enemy within 15cm: -1 Ld

\*This is for a tabletop feature such as an asteroid field, gas cloud, or planet. If a tabletop effect such as a radiation burst or solar flare is taking place this bonus applies anywhere on the tabletop. Only apply this bonus once, regardless of the number of celestial phenomena within range.

If the test is passed, the disengaging ship is removed from the tabletop and may not return during the game. If the test is failed the ship remains in play and may not fire, launch ordnance or attempt to use special orders (except Brace for Impact) during the rest of its turn. Note that both enemy ships and ordnance markers count against a vessel's leadership when it attempts to disengage. Furthermore, remember that a ship or squadron's leadership value cannot exceed 10 for any reason.

**Forced Disengagement:** Any ship or squadron that through any combination of effects is reduced to Leadership 1 or less must attempt to disengage or move off of the closest table edge (if possible).

**Squadrons disengaging:** if any escort in a squadron disengages, the rest of the squadron must immediately try to disengage, by any means available, and keep trying every later turn. Capital ships always disengage individually, using their own Ld. A capital ship that tries and fails drops out of its squadron for good and acts as a single ship.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Advanced Rules - Disengaging from Combat", p.38*

## 38. Victory Points

In many scenarios, the winner will be the player who scores the most victory points. Victory points are a measure of how much damage a fleet has inflicted on its enemy and are won by crippling and destroying enemy ships. Additional victory points can also be scored by achieving certain objectives, as detailed in the Victory Conditions section of the scenario, such as escaping an attack or attacking an enemy base. Victory points are earned as follows:

**Enemies Destroyed:** Each capital ship, escort squadron, and defence installation that is completely destroyed at the end of the game awards the opposing player a number of victory points equal to that unit/squadron's total points value, including the cost of any commanders or other upgrades on board. Note that capital ships and planetary defences (even those that behave like escorts) are always calculated separately for victory point purposes, even if part of a squadron.

**Enemies Crippled:** Each capital ship, escort squadron, and defence installation that is crippled at the end of the game awards the opposing player a number of victory points equal to 25% of that unit/squadron's total points value, including the cost of any commanders, upgrades (and so on) as normal. An escort squadron is considered to be crippled for this purpose if at least half its ships (rounding up) are destroyed. In a campaign game, units that start crippled must suffer at least one damage to count for this condition.

**Enemies Scattered:** Each non-crippled capital ship, escort squadron, and defence installation that has disengaged before the end of the game awards the opposing player a number of victory points equal to 10% of that unit/squadron's total points value, including the cost of any commanders, upgrades (and so on) as normal.

**Holding the Field:** At the end of the battle, the fleet that holds the field earns victory points for the number of hulks it captures. Your fleet holds the field at the end of the battle if there are no enemy vessels remaining (all have either disengaged or been destroyed) and your fleet has at least one operational ship on the table. If a fleet holds the field, the controlling player earns victory points equal to 50% of the total points value of each hulk on the table, both friendly and enemy. Note that this is in addition to any victory points awarded for actually destroying those ships.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Scenarios - Victory Points", p.79*

## 39. Terrain & Celestial Phenomena

### Setting Up the Battlefield

**Table:** a battlefield is usually 6'-8' long (about 1.8-2.4m) and 4'-6' wide (1.2-1.8m); on a smaller table, use proportionately smaller fleets. Any arrangement of celestial phenomena is acceptable as long as it is fair, and both players must agree to any piece that will dominate the battle (such as an asteroid belt across the whole table).

**Battlezones:** a system has six battlezones, from the Flare Region nearest the sun out to Deep Space. The scenario may set the battlezone; if the players have a choice, use one of these methods:

- *Player agreement:* agree on any battlezone the scenario allows.

- *Home-field advantage:* if the scenario needs a planet, the defender chooses any allowed battlezone.

- *Attack ratings:* each player secretly chooses a battlezone and adds its number to their fleet's attack rating. The higher total fights in the battlezone it chose.

**Setting the table:** use one of these methods:

- *Arbitrary setup:* one player places all the celestial phenomena, then the other picks a valid table edge to deploy from (ignoring scenario restrictions and roll-offs for deployment zones).

- *Random generation:* divide the table into 60cm squares and roll a D6 for each. On a 4+, roll on the battlezone's table below and place the feature at least partly in that square, without overlapping another feature. If the result is a tabletop effect, note it down instead. Players may use their own tables instead.

**Battlezone Phenomena Tables**

| D6 | 1. Flare Region | 2. Mercurial Zone | 3. Inner Biosphere |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solar flare | Solar flare | Roll a D6: 1-3 solar flare, 5-6 radiation burst† |
| 2 | Solar flare | Radiation burst | Asteroid field |
| 3 | Radiation burst | Asteroid field | D3 asteroid fields |
| 4 | Asteroid field | D3 gas/dust clouds | D3 gas/dust clouds |
| 5 | D3 gas/dust clouds | D3 gas/dust clouds | D3 gas/dust clouds |
| 6 | Planet (S/M) | Planet (S/M) | Planet (S/M) |

| D6 | 4. Primary Biosphere | 5. Outer Reaches | 6. Deep Space |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asteroid field | D3+1 asteroid fields | D3 asteroid fields |
| 2 | D3 asteroid fields | D3 asteroid fields | Asteroid field |
| 3 | Gas/dust cloud | D3 gas/dust clouds | D3 gas/dust clouds |
| 4 | D3 gas/dust clouds | Gas/dust cloud | Gas/dust cloud |
| 5 | Planet (S/M) | Planet (S/L) | Warp rift |
| 6 | Planet (S/M) | Planet (S/L) | Small planet (such as a rogue moon) |

**Planet:** (S/M) roll a D6, 1-5 small, 6 medium; (S/L) roll a D6, 1-3 small, 4-6 large. There is never more than one planet on the table: if a second is generated, re-roll. Check whether a planet has moons; a large planet may also have rings.

†The rulebook gives no result for a roll of 4 here.

### Planets

**Placement & Composition:** Planets are placed anywhere on the table or as described in the scenario being played. In addition, planets may have moons and/or rings. A medium planet usually has D3-1 moons, while a large planet usually has D6-2 moons. A large planet will also have D3 rings on a D6 roll of 5 or 6. The rules for moons and rings are described below.

**Typical Template Sizes**

| Size | Diameter | Gravity |
|---|---|---|
| Small | 0-15cm | 0-10cm |
| Medium | 16-25cm | 0-15cm |
| Large | 26-50cm | 0-30cm |

*Gravity well is measured from the template edge. Roughly: small = Mercury, Pluto or Mars; medium = Venus or Earth; large = Saturn or Jupiter.*

**Effects:** Planets are represented by a template or model placed on the tabletop. The template blocks line of sight for direct fire weapons, and any torpedoes that strike it are detonated. Hulks which drift into a planet are also destroyed. Ships may move 'through' a planet (passing over or under it). However, line of sight is not blocked to any such ships that are on or touching a planetary template, and torpedoes fired from within the template are removed when they reach the edge.

**Gravity Wells:** Furthermore, every planet is surrounded by an area of space where its gravitational pull is strong enough to affect shipping. This area is referred to as its gravity well. The gravity well extends out a set distance from the edge of the planet template and affects a ship's maneuvering. *Gravity well radius by planet size is given in the Typical Template Sizes table above.*

**High Orbit:** A ship within a planet's gravity well is normally in high orbit. While in high orbit, ships do not need to move, but remember that stationary vessels use the defences column on the Gunnery chart.

In addition, a ship may make a free turn when it begins and/or ends its move in high orbit. These free turns must be made towards the center of the planetary template, or up to 45 degrees in that direction (by the shortest possible angle), whichever is less. Turns made this way do not require any minimum movement distance. They can even be made when a ship cannot normally turn, such as when under All Ahead Full or Lock On special orders. Finally, they can also be combined with Come To New Heading special orders.

**Low Orbit:** Alternatively, ships within a planet's gravity well at the start of their turn may choose to enter low orbit. A ship that does so is removed from play and placed on a separate low orbit table. Ships which return to high orbit are placed touching the outer edge of the planet template.

**Fighting in Low Orbit (planetary assault scenarios):** In certain scenarios, ships can enter low orbit to attack a planet. Achieving low orbit is essential to any such attack, since a drop ship's range is very limited and any attempt to bombard ground forces from a greater distance is purely up to chance.

You will need a separate table (or section at one end of the main table) to represent low orbit. This does not need to be very large: 45-60cm wide by 90-135cm long should be sufficient. Nominate one long table edge as the 'planet' edge, representing the surface of the planet itself.

**Low Orbit Special Rules:** Ships in low orbit do not have to move and capital ships do not require a minimum distance before they can turn. To represent interference from the planet's gravity and the outer edges of its atmosphere, all gunnery weapons in low orbit suffer one column shift to the right. Furthermore, lances and nova cannons require a 4+ roll to fire, and torpedoes and mines may not be launched at all.

Ships that move within 45cm of the planet edge are gripped in the heart of the gravity well and must use their engines to keep them aloft. At the start of each player's turn, all ships within 45cm of the planet table edge fall directly towards the planet (without changing facing in any way). The distance that they move depends upon the size of the planet:

- Small: 5cm

- Medium: 8cm

- Large: 10cm

Any ship that moves off of the planet table edge in this way is totally destroyed. Escorts and transports which voluntarily move off the planet edge are said to have landed and count as disengaging. However, capital ships cannot land. If a ship in low orbit moves off from any other edge, it returns to high orbit. However, a ship may not enter and leave low orbit in the same turn, and any ordnance launched on the low orbit table cannot leave it at all.

**Moons:** Most planets have many small moons around them and most of these are no larger than generously sized asteroids. These rules are confined to dealing with larger moons several thousand kilometers in diameter.

**Placement:** Moons use templates up to 5cm in diameter. Each moon is placed 2D6x10cm from the planet's edge, in a direction determined by a scatter die (pointing from the center of the template). Note that moons do not move during the course of the game, as the time-scale for their orbit is much longer than that of a typical battle.

**Effects:** Moons are counted as small planets in all respects, including the effects of gravity wells and so forth. However, we recommend that players do not use the low orbit rules for moons unless fleets are very large and there is ample table space available.

**Ringed Planets:** Occasionally large planets will have rings made up of gas, dust and asteroids.

**Placement:** Each ring is D6cm wide and begins D6x5cm away from the planet's edge. Note that some may end up merging into one another, but that's fine. Use any materials or templates you prefer to represent them. If you have pieces with fixed sizes and materials, feel free to use those instead, rather than generating the size and type of rings randomly (this can speed up the setup process).

**Effects:** Roll a D6 to determine the type of each ring: a score of 1-4 generates a gas/dust ring, while a score of 5-6 generates an asteroid ring. These use the rules for gas/dust clouds and asteroid fields, respectively. Alternatively, you may simply assign a type to each ring instead, to fit with your available models.

### Asteroid Fields

Asteroid fields orbit most stars at varying distances. They are generally thought to be debris fragments left over from collisions between planets during the formation of a star system. Asteroid fields may also be left over after the destruction of a planet or moon, or represent an area of wreckage resulting from a space battle.

**Placement:** Each asteroid field is D3x5cm wide and D3x5cm long. Use rocks or gravel to represent them on the table, with the long direction running parallel to the sunward table edge.

**Effects:** Asteroid fields block line of sight for direct fire attacks such as lances, gunnery weapons, and nova cannon, and any torpedoes that strike one will automatically detonate. Hulks which drift into an asteroid field are also destroyed. Attack craft squadrons which move through an asteroid field are destroyed on a D6 roll of 6, just like for blast markers. Opposing vessels in an asteroid field may fire upon each other with battery-type weapons and lances, but at no more than 10cm range, and all weapons do so at half strength/firepower (and no column-shifts for close range). Crippled or braced ships in an asteroid field may not fire at all.

Furthermore, ships moving through an asteroid field, or coming into base contact with the edge of one, must first pass a leadership test to navigate it successfully. Ships on All Ahead Full special orders must test on 3D6 instead of 2D6. Due to their size, escorts may re-roll the result if they fail, but the second roll stands. A ship that fails this test suffers D6 damage from asteroid impacts (shields blocking damage normally). Note that blast markers are not placed when asteroid impacts overload shields, but the ship will still be slowed down by 5cm.

Squadrons test once for the whole group, just like any other leadership check. When a squadron of capital ships fails the test, each member that comes in base contact with the asteroid field in any way suffers D6 damage. On the other hand, when an escort squadron fails this test (even after re-rolling it), D6 damage must be distributed evenly among the escorts that actually contacted the asteroid field, in the order that the ships were moved.

Note that asteroid fields do not block damage from exploding ships for any reason - resolve explosions as though the field were not there.

**Firing on Asteroid Fields:** In rare instances, players may wish to fire upon an asteroid field. To do so, a ship or squadron must first pass a leadership test (regardless of range, and even if it is the only possible target). Shooting at an asteroid field is resolved normally, counting the field as ordnance. For each hit inflicted, place a blast marker in contact with the edge of the field, as close as possible to the direction of shooting (without overlapping other markers).

### Gas and Dust Clouds

Gas and dust clouds represent areas of space with a notably greater density of (mostly) hydrogen gas or tiny particles of matter. These clouds may be fragments left over from the formation of stars and planets, the outer fringes of nebulae or protostars, or even gasses ejected by solar flares. They represent a moderate navigational hazard to shipping: basic shielding is sufficient to prevent damage but ships must reduce speed to pass through them. Gas and dust clouds impair targeting by macrobatteries and may destroy ordnance however, making them useful areas to exploit in ship-to-ship combat.

**Placement:** Each gas/dust cloud is D6x2cm wide and D6x5cm long. Use flock or cotton wool to represent them on the table, with the long direction running parallel to the sunward table edge.

**Effects:** To ships in base contact, gas and dust clouds have the same effect as a single blast marker in all respects (such as for shooting, movement, shields, etc). Note that if a ship with no shields whatsoever (not those whose shields are just overloaded) takes damage from a gas or dust cloud and explodes, the explosion is resolved at the point where the ship made contact with the feature.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Celestial Phenomena - Tabletop Features", p.52-54*

### Warp Rifts

**Placement:** a warp rift is D3×5cm wide and D3×10cm long, in any orientation.

**Effects:** warp rifts block line of sight for direct fire like an asteroid field, and torpedoes that hit one detonate. Attack craft that move into a rift are destroyed, and hulks that drift into one are lost (they cannot be salvaged after the battle).

A ship moving into a warp rift must pass a Leadership test on 3D6 to navigate it. If it passes, place it up to 2D6×10cm from the rift, facing any direction. If it fails, it is lost in the warp and leaves the battle. After the game, roll a D6 for each ship lost in the warp: on a 1 it counts as destroyed, on 2-6 as disengaged.

Defences must be placed at least 30cm from any warp rift.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Celestial Phenomena - Setting Up the Battlefield, Battlezones, Warp Rifts", pp.49-53*

## 40. Celestial: Sun, Solar Flares & Radiation

### Sunward Table Edge

Tabletop features are generally placed in relation to the nearest star. This is because nearly everything caught in the inconceivably gross gravitational pull of a star will be in some kind of orbit around it. When placing tabletop features, always start by determining which table edge is closest to the nearest star, described as 'sunward' in Battlefleet Gothic. To do so, roll a D6 and compare the result to the diagram in the book. Once you have established which way is sunward, you can start to place celestial phenomena.

**Fighting Sunward (Tabletop Effects):** In close proximity, an opposing ship with the sun behind it is easier to pick out and track using reflection surveyors and image capture devices.

**Effects:** In the outer parts of a solar system, the light from the distant star has no effect on combat. In battles taking place from the Flare Region to the Inner Biosphere, the intense light from the sun provides the following effects: Any gunnery weapons fired towards the sunward table edge double the normal number of column shifts for long and short range. To determine if you are shooting sunward, place the bearing compass over the firing ship with the arrows parallel to the sunward table edge. If the target is within the abeam arc on the sunward side, the vessel is firing sunward.

For example, a cruiser firing its macrobatteries at a sunward target 40cm away would suffer two column shifts to the right instead of one. Likewise, a ship firing at point blank range (within 15cm) gains two column shifts left rather than only one.

### Solar Flares

Most stars periodically release explosive bursts of energy over small areas of their surface. Of course small, in solar terms, means areas hundreds of millions of kilometers across! These huge flares of energy rush outward at tremendous speeds, flooding the vicinity with highly charged particles and magnetic shock waves. A shielded vessel can find its protection virtually overwhelmed by these events and a vessel without shields is sure to suffer damage.

**Effects:** At the start of each turn, roll a D6 per solar flare generated on the celestial phenomena table. If any dice score a 6, a solar flare occurs. Note that a solar flare may only occur once per game - afterwards, no further rolls are made in subsequent turns. When a solar flare occurs, each ship on the table has one blast marker placed in contact with its base, as close as possible to the sunward table edge. Any vessel without shields (whether overloaded, collapsed, etc) suffers one automatic hit and will take critical damage on a roll of 4+ (rather than 6). Finally, roll a D6 for each ordnance marker on the table - on a score of 4 or more it is removed from play.

### Radiation Bursts

As well as solar flares and often in conjunction with them, a sun will frequently emit bursts of radiation, including electromagnetic and radio waves. These temporarily scramble any communications traffic between ships and even disrupt ship-board comm-nets. Commanding a ship under these conditions is extremely difficult, and for this reason most captains assiduously avoid the flare region and mercurial zone of the local star.

**Effects:** At the start of each turn, roll a D6 per radiation burst generated on the celestial phenomena table. If any dice score a 5 or more, a radiation burst occurs (limit one per turn). If this happens, roll a D6 to see what the interference level will be. All ships on the table reduce their Leadership value by that value for the duration of the turn. For example, if a radiation burst occurs and a 3 is rolled for the interference level, all ships suffer -3 to their leadership. In addition to the reduced leadership value for the interference, Fleet Commanders may only use their re-rolls on command checks for their own ship or squadron during radiation bursts.

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, "Tabletop Features / Tabletop Effects" - Sunward p.52; Fighting Sunward, Solar Flares & Radiation Bursts p.55*
