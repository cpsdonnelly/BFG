# Necrons (Xenos p.38-51)

## Faction Rules

Source book: *BFG:XR - Ships of the Xenos*, "The Necrons in Battlefleet Gothic", "Necron Space Combat", "Necron Dynasty Upgrades" and "Scenarios & Campaigns". Printed pp.39-41 (PDF pp.43-45). Reminder: for this book, printed page + 4 = PDF page.

**Leadership & Special Orders**

**Necron Leadership:** Although they are essentially fully automated, Necron ships must still generate leadership normally.

**All Ahead Full:** Necron propulsion systems are capable of interstellar travel without the need to enter the Warp. This 'inertialess drive' is fired whenever *All Ahead Full* orders are issued; instead of obeying the normal rules for this order, the ship gains D6x10cm additional movement distance, and may make a turn for every 20cm it travels. Any other rules concerning *All Ahead Full* special orders still apply normally.

**Brace for Impact:** If a Necron vessel uses *Brace for Impact* special orders, its reactive hull save is improved to 2+, but its stealth properties are instantly compromised; the vessel's Armour value is reduced to 4+. This effect replaces the 4+ save usually granted to ships using *Brace for Impact* special orders, however all other effects (such as halving weapon strength, etc) are still applied as normal.

**Lock On:** Only a Necron vessel's lightning arc and particle whips are affected by *Lock On* special orders; any attacks made by nightmare fields, star pulse generators, and/or portals receive no benefit.

**Reload Ordnance:** Necron attack craft are usually only deployed for surface engagements. Likewise, Necrons do not use torpedoes at all. Thus, Necron ships may not use *Reload Ordnance* special orders, as they have no need to do so. (The Necron tomb complex is the exception; see Necron Ship Special Rules.)

### Reactive Hulls

Necron ships do not employ conventional shields of any kind. Necron vessels are immune to leadership, damage, and movement penalties from solar flares, radiation, gas clouds, and blast markers. They also gain a special save against incoming damage to represent the hull's adaptive qualities. This save is 6+ for escorts, 5+ for cruisers, and 4+ for battleships, applying any time a *Brace for Impact* save could be used (including against hit and run attacks). Finally, all Necron ships may repair critical damage on a roll of 4+ rather than only on a roll of 6.

### Deadly Awakening

**Power Conservation:** At the beginning of each game, all Necron capital ships in the fleet must conserve power. Ships conserving power function as if they were crippled for all game purposes except scoring and other victory conditions. In addition, such vessels will automatically phase out if turned into a drifting or burning hulk; simply remove them instead.

**Escalating Threat:** The Necron fleet starts each game at alert level 1, before any modifiers.\* During the game, whenever an enemy ship or ordnance marker inflicts an unsaved hit (or hit-and-run raid) on a Necron vessel, the Necron fleet gains 1 alert level, up to a maximum of 5. Additionally, if the opposing fleet begins the game with a greater points limit worth of ships and/or defences on the table, the Necron fleet starts with +2 alert levels. Lastly, the presence of a hostile Space Hulk, Proteus Hive Ship, Planet Killer, or Activated Blackstone Fortress will also increase the Necrons' starting alert level by +1.

\*Ships of the Xenos does not state a starting alert level; the author has confirmed that the Necron fleet starts at alert level 1.

**Awakening:** At the start of each Necron turn, note the fleet's current alert level and look up the corresponding row in the table below. Each Necron capital ship whose Leadership value equals or exceeds the indicated threshold (including any modifiers that would apply to its command checks, such as for 'Enemy Contacts') may awaken.

| Alert Level | Ld Required |
|---|---|
| 1 | 10+ |
| 2 | 9+ |
| 3 | 8+ |
| 4 | 7+ |
| 5 | No Restriction |

Awakened vessels cease conserving power and operate normally for the rest of the game (even if their Leadership is later reduced), but are worth double the normal number of victory points. Note that this applies to Necron hulks left on the table as well, meaning that a player who destroys an awakened Necron capital ship can potentially earn up to 300% of its total cost in victory points (200% for destroying it, plus an extra 100% for capturing the hulk if they hold the field at the end of the game).

**Scenario Reinforcements:** Necron capital ships which arrive as reinforcements after the first turn of the game are always treated as awakened, regardless of the fleet's alert level.

### Without a Trace

The Necrons seldom leave anything behind after a battle, even when defeated. If in danger, they typically prefer to phase out and disengage rather than fight to the end, and will try to destroy any of their own ships unable to escape, rather than let them be captured. At the end of its movement phase, a Necron ship may choose to automatically disengage. This does not require a leadership check; the vessel simply vanishes. Should a Necron vessel actually be destroyed and remain as a hulk, other friendly Necron ships may fire upon it by passing a target priority check. Necron hulks have 4+ Armour, as the stealth properties of their hulls are compromised.

**Scoring:** Necron escorts are scored normally for all disengagement conditions, and the VP flowchart for capital ships has been removed (Xenos change log, p.127).

### Necron Weapons

**Nightmare Field:** A nightmare field targets one enemy capital ship or escort squadron within 20cm, which must then take a leadership test. If it passes, the attack has no effect. If the test is failed, the target's Leadership is reduced by 1 for the rest of the game and it (or its squadron, if any) cannot use any special orders until after the end of its next turn, including *Brace for Impact*. Furthermore, when activating a nightmare field, any enemy attack craft and boarding torpedoes within 20cm are destroyed on a roll of 4+ (resolved individually for each marker, even if part of a wave), regardless of the outcome of the target's leadership test. Necron vessels may not use their nightmare field while crippled or on any special orders except *Lock On*.

**Star Pulse Generator:** When used, a star pulse generator makes a single roll to-hit against the nearest Armour value of each non-Necron ship within 20cm. (It cannot damage Necron ships, even if they are enemies.) Furthermore, when activating a star pulse generator, any enemy ordnance markers within 20cm are destroyed on a roll of 4+ (resolved individually for each marker, even if part of a wave). Necron vessels may not fire their star pulse generators while crippled or on any special orders except *Lock On*.

**Particle Whip:** A particle whip functions in the same way as a regular lance weapon, except that any roll to-hit that scores a '6' bypasses the target's shields (as well as any holofields, shadowfields, or other defence besides *Brace for Impact*), affecting the targeted ship directly. If multiple dice score hits, the attacker chooses the order to apply the results.

**Lightning Arc:** Lightning arcs function as weapons batteries that treat all non-defence targets as closing on the gunnery table (though they must still roll against the Armour value of the nearest facing). In addition, lightning arcs ignore all gunnery-related effects of holofields and shadowfields (they do not suffer a right column-shift, but also cannot place tracking marks, etc). Lastly, a lightning arc with multiple firing arcs may split its firepower between them (in any proportions) without requiring a target priority test. In this case, it must shoot the closest valid target in each arc. Note that all firepower directed at each chosen target must be resolved in a single attack.

**Portals:** Necron ships equipped with portals replace their normal teleport attack with a number of portal attacks as shown in their profile. These are resolved exactly like standard teleport attacks, with the following exceptions:

- Portals may be used against targets with more hits than the attacker.

- Portals may be used while crippled or on special orders.

- Penalties that reduce the effectiveness of a ship's direct fire weapons (crippling, special orders, etc) will apply to its portal strength as well. However, portals always round their strength down instead of up.

**Remastered:** Battlefleet Gothic Remastered treats the nightmare field and star pulse generator as area-effect weapons that do not aim and are not directed at a particular target; they are not blocked by line of sight obstructions such as hulks, minefields or celestial phenomena, and cannot be saved against by holofields (Remastered p.64). It also states that Necron portals are not teleport attacks in the normal manner (Remastered p.92).

### Dynasty Upgrades

These upgrades are found only in Necron Dynasty fleets; costs are in the Necron Dynasty Fleet List.

- *Bloodswarm Nanoscarabs:* A ship carrying bloodswarm nanoscarabs adds +1 to the results of its portal attacks.

- *Reciprocating Tesla Pylons:* A ship with reciprocating tesla pylons increases its total lightning arc firepower by 50%, but must divide its firepower equally between each firing arc (rounding down), even if a given arc has no possible targets. Apply this effect before any other modifications.

### Critical Hits & Campaigns

**Critical Hits:** Necron vessels use the Necron Critical Hits Table (Appendix 3, p.226). Note that some results will inflict a leadership penalty on the ship, as shown in the 'Extra Damage' column. These penalties are applied each time the system is hit, just like any other extra damage, and cannot be removed during the game, even if the system is repaired. If playing in a campaign, these leadership penalties are automatically repaired at the end of each game, in the same way as critical damage results.

**Campaign Refits & Crew Skills:** In a campaign, a Necron fleet does not earn refits or crew skills. Whenever a ship would earn a crew skill or an appeal for a refit is granted, the fleet gains one repair point instead.

*Source: Ships of the Xenos, "Necron Special Rules", pp.39-41*

Necron ships have no shields; the Sh column shows the reactive hull save (RH), which applies wherever a *Brace for Impact* save could be used. Lightning arcs are weapons batteries, particle whips are lances, star pulse generators are area effect within 20cm, and portals replace the ship's teleport attack.

**Arcs:** (F) = one mount firing forward. (L/R), (L/F/R) = one lightning arc or particle whip mount that can fire into any of those arcs; a lightning arc may split its firepower between its arcs, shooting the closest target in each (see Necron Weapons).

### Necron Ship Special Rules

#### Reaper Class Grand Harvest Ship (Xenos p.43)

**Nebuloscope Pylons:** Every vessel in the fleet gains an extra +1Ld bonus to its command checks from the 'Enemy Contacts' modifier in addition to the standard effect (this bonus is not cumulative, regardless of the number of nebuloscope pylons in the fleet). Nebuloscope pylons will function while conserving power, but not if the ship is actually crippled due to damage suffered.

#### Shroud Class Light Cruiser (Xenos p.45)

**Negative Energy Veil:** A ship with a negative energy veil is ignored for the purpose of the 'Enemy Contacts' modifier on opposing vessels' command checks and cannot be detected by mines or minefields (though it will still be attacked by enemy mines in base contact). Additionally, while conserving power, the ship cannot be fired upon unless the attacker first passes a target priority check (even if it is the closest, or only, target).

**Nebuloscope Pylons:** Every vessel in the fleet gains an extra +1Ld bonus to its command checks from the 'Enemy Contacts' modifier in addition to the standard effect (this bonus is not cumulative, regardless of the number of nebuloscope pylons in the fleet). Nebuloscope pylons will function while conserving power, but not if the ship is actually crippled due to damage suffered.

**Special Refits:** When included in a Necron Dynasty fleet, a Shroud class light cruiser may remove its nebuloscope pylons to choose any combination of the following options at no extra cost. (If both Khopesh and Cartouche configuration are chosen, the resulting particle whips are combined into a single FP2 weapon.)

- *War Configuration:* the ship exchanges its negative energy veil for +2 Hits and +1 Turrets.

- *Khopesh Configuration:* the ship's lightning arc is restricted to firing Left / Right only in exchange for a particle whip (45cm, FP1, L/F/R).

- *Cartouche Configuration:* the ship's lightning arc is reduced to FP8 in exchange for a particle whip (45cm, FP1, L/F/R).

#### Dirge Class Raider (Xenos p.46)

A Dirge class raider may be used as a convoy or system ship in any scenario where such units are allowed. Dirges used this way must follow the rules for support units, reduce their base speed to 25cm, and will gain a transport capacity of 1. In all other respects they remain Necron ships.

#### Deathseed Minefield and Deathseed Mine (Xenos p.47)

A deathseed minefield follows all the normal rules for minefields as described in the main rulebook, except that in all cases it will generate deathseed mines rather than normal mines, including when it attacks a detected ship inside the field. Deathseed mines follow all the normal rules for orbital mines as described in the main rulebook and the standard Fleet Support list, with the exception that damage from deathseed mines will ignore a target's shields in the same manner as other ordnance such as torpedoes, bombers, etc.

#### Necron Pylon (Xenos p.48)

A Necron pylon is a planetary defence choice for a Necron fleet. It follows all the rules for ground facilities found in the main rulebook, and may be placed overlapping with a Necron tomb complex.

#### Necron Tomb Complex (Xenos p.49)

**Using a Tomb Complex:** A Necron tomb complex is a planetary defence choice available to the Necrons. It counts as a ground facility in all respects and follows all the rules for such units found in the main rulebook. A Necron player cannot have more than one tomb complex.

**Leadership & Special Orders:** Like other planetary defences, a tomb complex has a nominal leadership of 7. It may only use *Reload Ordnance* (ignoring the usual Necron restrictions) and *Brace for Impact* special orders. Bracing affects a tomb complex just like it would a Necron ship.

**Deployment:** When a tomb complex is deployed, mark a line 2D3x5cm along the table edge representing the planet's surface on the low orbit table. (If necessary, use asteroids or other terrain markers to denote this area.) This region counts as the 'base' of the tomb complex. All measurements for the complex are made to or from the nearest point along this portion of the table edge, in the same way that a ship normally measures distance to a table edge.

**Weapons Systems:** A tomb complex may fire its lightning arc and/or launch attack craft from any point along its length. Note that the lightning arc may be fired from a different point than from where any ordnance was launched, and vice versa.

**Necron Ordnance:** The Necrons use the following attack craft: Night Scythes, which follow the rules for assault boats; Doom Scythes, which follow the rules for bombers; Night Shrouds, which follow the rules for torpedo bombers.

**Damage Control:** A tomb complex may re-roll the dice to repair critical damage, in addition to succeeding on a 4+ like other Necron units.

**Buried Citadel:** A tomb complex cannot be destroyed. Instead, for each point of damage inflicted upon a tomb complex, place a blast marker in contact along its length, as close to the firer as possible. For each blast marker in contact, reduce the firepower/strength of the tomb's armaments by 1 (to a minimum of zero). Apply any critical hits normally. Note that a tomb complex removes D6 blast markers in contact at the end of each turn, just like other stationary defences.

**Tomb Raiding:** A tomb complex does not have any effect on the scoring of assault points or the successful extermination of a planet; any invaders will treat an attack on a tomb world just like any other planetary assault or Exterminatus scenario. However, a commander may attack a tomb complex directly. When an opponent lands a ship on a tomb complex, he or she may roll a D6. On a result of 5+, that player places D3 blast markers in contact with the tomb complex and (if the scenario uses assault points) scores +1 assault point. On a result of 4 or less, the landing ship counts as being destroyed! If a capital ship (which cannot land) crashes into the complex, it is treated as ramming the tomb (counting the tomb as having 10 hits and being rammed from the front). Note that damage to the crashing ship is resolved before removing it, meaning that catastrophic damage effects (such as a plasma drive overload) will affect the tomb complex normally.

**Fleet Composition (Harvest and Dynasty Fleet Lists)**

Two fleet lists (*Xenos p.50-51*): the Harvest fleet (attack rating 4) and the Dynasty fleet (attack rating 3). Neither will ever provide ships to or take ships from any other fleet list as reserves or allies. Both must include at least one capital ship, and may include up to twenty-four escorts in squadrons of two to six ships; a squadron cannot have more than four two-hit escorts. Necrons cannot use the generic fleet support units (see Support Assets); their planetary defences are listed above.
