# Campaigns

## Overview

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, printed p.127.*

At some stage all wargamers contemplate running a campaign. This is understandable, after all, battles don't take place in isolation; rather, they are part of an ongoing war. A campaign allows you to fight such a war by linking battles together, so that the results of one battle will have an effect on the future ones you fight. Over the course of the campaign, you will be able to develop cunning strategies to conquer whole sub-sectors and watch your fleet develop as ships' crews hone their skills in battle. Will your commander rise to new heights of glory or fall into the depths of ignominy? Embark on a campaign and find out!

*Flavour text:* I ask myself what it means to be one of the Dark Angels. Is it to hunt the Fallen, chasing shadows through the dark places of the galaxy? Is it to pursue our quest at any expense, forgoing all other oaths and duties? Is it to lie, to hide and to plot so that others will never know of our shame? Is it to keep our own brethren unacquainted with the truth of our past, the legacy we all share in? Or is it to be a Space Marine? Is it to follow the path laid down by the Emperor and Lion El'Johnson at the founding of this great Imperium of Man? To protect mankind, to purge the alien, cleanse the unclean?, Interrogator-Chaplain Boreas

## Running a Campaign

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, printed p.128.*

As I hope you can tell, campaigns can be a lot of fun and they are not all that difficult to run either. To help you, we've developed a map-based campaign system based on our own experiences of running campaigns over the years, but you really should feel free to modify and change what's here to suit your own tastes. For example, playing a campaign offers great opportunities to try out new rules or new classes of ship that you've invented, or you could start thinking of adding rules for fighting land battles and boarding actions using the Warhammer 40,000 and Epic 40,000 rules. The possibilities really are endless.

**Getting Started.** In order to run a campaign you'll need at least two players to represent the main factions opposing one another in the campaign. You may also include any number of players whose factions will act as non-aligned raiders. Raiders will fight battles normally but do not capture systems, making them ideal for players that wish to participate but cannot commit to a fixed schedule.

Once you've got your players together you need to pick one of the sub-sectors on the sub-sector maps as the location for your campaign to take place. For your first campaign we recommend starting off with one of the smaller sub-sectors, unless you have a large number of players (i.e. more than eight) taking part in the campaign. You'll probably need to photocopy the sub-sector map you've decided to use (or draw it by hand) to keep track of which player controls each system.

Next you need to get all the players together for a "campaign briefing". The most important thing you'll need to go over at this point is the rules for the campaign, to make sure that everybody knows what's going on. You should then decide how long the campaign is to last. For your first campaign we recommend playing for one month of real time and on the whole it's best to keep campaigns short, after all, you can always start another one!

Once this has been decided, players can pick their starting systems and design their fleets using the rules described below, then you can start fighting battles. However, it is usually a good idea to set a regular time and place for campaign meetings, and also to elect a "campaign arbitrator" who is in charge of making sure that the campaign runs smoothly. You might also want to consider putting together a campaign newsletter, in which case you'll need to decide who will be its editor.

**Starting Systems.** At the start of the campaign, many of the systems in the sub-sector will be neutral. As the campaign progresses, this will change and players will gain control of systems that will pay them tithes and help repair their ships.

*Designer's Note: In reality, every star system starts nominally under the control of the Imperium, but as planetary governors tend to go their own way until reminded of their obligations, they are for all intents and purposes neutral.*

Each regular player starts with one of the systems in the sub-sector under their control. Roll off to decide the order in which players pick their starting system, and record who controls what. Raider players start with a hidden base instead, and should write down secretly in which system it is located (they can pick a system controlled by a regular player).

**Starting Fleets & the Fleet Register.** Regular fleets begin the campaign with 2,000 points and raider fleets start with 1,500 points. Important: you must have models to represent all of the ships in your fleet, in case they all end up in a big battle! If your fleet doesn't add up to 2,000 points, then just use what you've got available. (If players wish, the campaign may be played at lower points values in general, just remember to start raiders at 75% of the regular fleets' points values.)

A starting fleet is chosen from one of your faction's fleet lists as normal (except your fleet commander, see The Commander, below). As you add to your fleet during a campaign, you must obey the restrictions of your fleet list except where otherwise specified in the campaign rules. Record your starting fleet on your fleet registry, described below: each player has their own fleet registry, used to keep notes and dates of when each capital ship or escort squadron was commissioned (i.e. purchased from their fleet list) along with class of ship, when it takes its fleet trials (i.e. it's assembled, painted and its Leadership rating is rolled for), its campaign history, and its loss if and when these events occur.

If a vessel is used in battle before its fleet trials are complete (i.e. it's not fully assembled and painted) it will suffer a critical hit on a D6 roll of 1 every time it uses special orders. Ships which are added to the fleet register after the start of the campaign suffer a -1 modifier to their Leadership due to their hasty training and less experienced officers.

When players fight battles they must pick their forces according to the restrictions of their fleet list, but may only use forces which are actually shown on their fleet register.

## The Commander

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, printed p.129.*

Each player's fleet begins with a commander (or an appropriately named leader for their faction) who will represent the player himself (or herself). Over the course of the campaign, your commander will collect renown points and gain in rank, and at the end of the campaign the commander with the highest renown is the winner.

Your campaign commander fills the role of a fleet commander in your fleet list, but does not cost any points and cannot purchase any of the usual options shown in the list's Fleet Commander section. (They will instead receive bonuses as they develop during the campaign, see the Promotion tables, below.)

Once you've filled in your fleet register, you need to fill in the details of your commander. He or she will need a name, and starts off with 1 point of renown and one re-roll. In addition, you must pick one ship in your fleet as their flagship. If this ship is involved in a battle then "you" are there, and you may use the commander's re-roll or any other special abilities he or she may have in the battle. If the flagship is not there then you may not use the commander's reroll or abilities.

If the flagship is destroyed, then it is assumed that your commander manages to escape somehow. He or she may not have any further effect in that game, but you may choose a new flagship for them once the game is over.

**Fighting Battles.** In order for things to happen during the campaign players must fight battles! At its simplest level all players have to do is challenge any other player that is in the campaign to a game and if they agree then the game uses the additional campaign rules below as well as the rules used for a normal game.

The only problem with this is that it can lead to some odd situations where fleets of the same faction attack each other to gain control of systems. Thus, it's best if players of the same faction are prohibited from attacking each other. If you have a lot of fleets from a specific faction in the campaign (or, indeed, only a single faction), then it is best to split them into loyalists and rebels, the latter being assumed to have committed some terrible act of betrayal!

Anyway, however you decide to do it, you need to challenge other players in the campaign to a game. The game is arranged in exactly the same manner as a one-off game; simply agree on a time and a place and go for it. You can play as many or as few campaign games as you like, all you need to do is find an opponent.

Note that opposing players do not have to agree to play just because you happened to have challenged them to a battle, but you may want to penalize players that refuse to play games with anybody for long periods of time and spoil the campaign for the other players. For example, if a player doesn't play any games for (say) a fortnight then they lose a point of renown, and if they don't play any games for a month then one of their systems (randomly selected) rebels and is turned over to another player.

**The Campaign Turn.** Campaign games include a number of extra steps which take place at the beginning and end of the game, as described below. Unless stated otherwise all of the normal Battlefleet Gothic rules apply in a campaign game.

Each time that two players have a game they both get what is known as a campaign turn. A campaign turn uses the following sequence of play:

1\. **Build Up:** Determine Attacker & Defender; Roll for Incoming Orders; Select Target System; Pick Fleets

2\. **Fight Battle**

3\. **Aftermath:** Spoils of War; Adjust Renown; Promotions & Demotions; Ship Experience; Repairs & Withdrawals; Make Appeals

## Build-Up

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, printed p.130.*

The build-up stage takes place at the start of the battle, before either player deploys or picks their fleet. In this stage you decide where the battle will be, what type of scenario to play, and what size of fleets to use. The stage has a number of steps that need to be carried out in the following order: 1. Determine Attacker & Defender; 2. Roll for Incoming Orders; 3. Pick Fleets.

**1. Determine Attacker & Defender.** Using the fleets' attack ratings as described on p.78 (above), determine who will be the attacker and who will be the defender. The player who controls less total systems does not need to spread their forces as thinly as their opponent, and thus adds +1 to the result(s) of their dice. Similarly, the player who controls more systems in total must spread their forces thinly, reducing their fleet size by 10 points for each additional system they hold over their opponent.

**2. Roll for Incoming Orders.** Although fleet commanders have a large measure of autonomy, higher command still sends down orders telling them what they must achieve next. In effect, the players get to decide what happens in the battles, but receive orders that direct the strategy they must follow, and the type of battles they will fight. To reflect this, the attacker must roll a D6 to determine what type and size of scenario is to be played.

| D6 Roll | Orders |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | Raid (500-750 points) |
| 3-6 | Battle (750-1500 points) |

If one player's commander has at least 21 Renown, that player may choose whether to fight a battle or a raid, even if they are the defender. If both players' commanders have at least 21 Renown, the attacker rolls for the scenario type normally.

After determining whether to fight a raid or battle, roll on the appropriate table below to determine the specific scenario to be played:

**Raids**

| D6 Roll | Scenario |
|---|---|
| 1 | The Raiders |
| 2 | Blockade Run |
| 3 | Convoy |
| 4 | Rearguard Attack |
| 5 | Navigate the Storm |
| 6 | Destroy the Docks |

**Battles**

| D6 Roll | Scenario |
|---|---|
| 1 | Exterminatus! |
| 2 | Alien Intervention |
| 3 | Surprise Attack |
| 4 | Planetary Assault |
| 5 | Escalating Engagement |
| 6 | Fleet Engagement |

Once the scenario has been determined, the attacker chooses the system where the game will take place. The system must be connected to one already under the attacker's control (via warp route on the sector diagram). If the attacker controls no systems, any may be chosen.

Having established the scenario and location, both players must agree on a points limit for the game. The chosen limit must abide by any restrictions in the scenario's rules (as well as the range shown in the Incoming Orders table above, if no value is specified). If the fight takes place in a system that is uninhabited, the defender reduces his or her fleet size by 100 points due to lack of supply lines.

*Raiders:* Note that raider fleets never engage in full battles. Instead, if one or both players are acting as raiders, treat results of 3-6 on the Incoming Orders table as a major raid rather than a battle. A major raid uses the Raids table to determine the scenario but will have a points limit of 750-1500 points like a standard battle.

**3. Pick Fleets.** Both players must now pick their fleets using ships from their fleet register. The total value of the ships you pick may not exceed the points total you and your opponent have decided for the scenario you are to play. Note that you can't change the details of ships on the register or adjust their points value at this stage, the ships you pick must come from your register and not be changed in any way. With the build-up completed all you need do now is roll for a sub-plot and then get fighting!

**Hidden Bases:** Raider fleets never get to capture systems, but instead have a secret base. If the opponent of a raider fleet is the attacker and has at least 21 renown points, that player may attempt to find and attack the secret base. To do this, they must roll a D6 and multiply the result by 10. If the result is less than the raider player's renown, the secret base is found and remains revealed until the raider player moves it. Otherwise, it stays hidden and the players choose a normal scenario to play.

A secret raider base cannot be attacked if it is in a system controlled by another regular player. If this prevents it from being attacked on the campaign turn it is discovered, play a normal game instead. Assuming it is found and is a valid target, the attacker chooses either the Planetary Assault or Exterminatus scenario to represent the attack on the base. If they win, the base is destroyed and may no longer be used.

## Aftermath

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, printed p.131.*

Once you have fought a campaign game and know who has won, you come to probably the most important stage: the aftermath. This is where you find out what effect the game you have just played has on the campaign map, as well as the effect it has had on the ships and crew that took part. This stage has a number of steps, which need to be carried out in the following order: 1. Spoils of War; 2. Adjust Renown; 3. Promotions & Demotions; 4. Ship Experience; 5. Repairs & Withdrawals; 6. Appeals.

**1. Spoils of War.** Whenever an attacker wins a battle, he or she may claim control of the system if it is connected by a warp route to one he or she already controls. The system can only be taken over by the player that won the battle if it previously belonged to the player that was defeated, or if it was previously neutral. Whenever an attacker wins a raid, he or she may count the system where the raid took place as his or her own for the rest of this campaign turn (which is important, since the systems you control affect your ability to repair ships, etc). After the campaign turn ends, control of the system reverts to normal.

**2. Adjust Renown.** Renown is a measure of the total fame (or infamy) enjoyed by you, the fleet commander. Players start the campaign with 1 point of renown and can gain or lose renown as shown on the Renown table below. Renown is very important, at the end of the campaign, the player that has earned the most renown is the winner! Note that you can gain or lose renown even if your commander was not present during the scenario (i.e. their flagship did not take part).

**Renown Table**

| Gained For | Amount |
|---|---|
| Winning a battle or major raid | +2 |
| Winning a raid | +1 |
| Victory points earned | +1 per 100 VPs (rounding up) |
| Sub-plots | Varies |
| Each hulk captured by holding the field | +1 |
| Fought against a commander with higher renown | +1 |
| Fought against a fleet worth more points and lost | +1 |
| Fought against a fleet worth more points and won | +2 |

| Lost For | Amount |
|---|---|
| Losing a battle | -1 |
| Losing a raid | -1 |
| Each capital ship lost | -1 |
| Sub-plots | Varies |

*Note: A commander can never be reduced below 1 renown point (you may only be renowned as a poor commander, but you will be renowned nonetheless).*

*Flavour text:* "There's not nearly enough. Whatever foul work the Arch-enemy does here must be stopped at all costs, but do not fool yourself Governor; this world will die. It just remains to be seen how many of us it takes with it.", Inquisitor Barzano

*Note: Upgrades granted by a commander's renown rank are chosen when the rank is achieved and do not change during the campaign. For example, a Chaos Overlord has two Marks of Chaos (one chosen at 6 renown and the other at 21 renown). ## 4. Ship Experience

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, printed p.134.*

As the campaign progresses, ships (or rather, their crews) will gain experience. This is represented by increasing their Leadership characteristic, and by giving them special "crew skills". On the other hand a ship that is badly damaged is likely to have lost a sizable proportion of its experienced crewmen, which will reduce its effectiveness, while a ship that is destroyed will have to be replaced by a new or salvaged vessel with a very inexperienced crew.

**Gaining Experience:** Ships which fought in a battle and were not crippled or destroyed roll 2D6. If the roll is higher than their Leadership rating, then either their Leadership may be improved by +1 point (up to a maximum of 10) or the ship may roll on the Crew Skills table for their faction (Imperial or Xenos), in the Appendix. You may choose which option to take, unless the ship has a Leadership of 6 or 7, in which case you must choose to increase the ship's Leadership.

**Crippled Ships:** Vessels that become crippled in a battle lose -1 Leadership (to a minimum of 6). Note that crew skills aren't lost for being crippled, even if the ship's Leadership is reduced to 6 or 7.

**Destroyed Ships:** Vessels which are destroyed (i.e. reduced to 0 damage points) must be replaced with a new ship of the same class. Change their name on your fleet register. The new ship has a Leadership of 6, no crew skills, and any refits that have been taken are lost (the rules for refits follow later on).

**Escort Squadrons:** Escort squadrons gain and lose Leadership and skills in the same way as ships. Escort squadrons which suffer 50% or greater casualties are considered crippled for the purpose of experience, while those that are wiped out are considered destroyed.

## 5. Repairs & Withdrawals

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, printed p.134.*

In a campaign, vessels that have suffered damage must be repaired, and it is the number of systems a player controls that determines just how much damage can be fixed. Sometimes the systems under your control won't be able to repair all the damage your fleet has suffered, in which case you can either withdraw the ships and send them to be repaired outside the sub-sector, or you can let them limp on as they are until you have time to repair them.

**Repairs:** Each system a player controls may repair a number of damage points. This varies depending on the type of system and your renown. The number of damage points different systems can repair is shown below. Renown is important because it helps with recruiting or press-ganging extra crew, claiming resources and time in dock etc. Note that all critical hits are repaired automatically, including ones which may not be repaired during a battle (i.e. Bridge Smashed and Shields Collapse). Also remember that if the attacker won a raid he may count the enemy system where the raid took place as his own for this turn. You can use Repair points to bring escort squadrons back up to strength, in which case each member is worth its Hits value.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-5 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| 6-10 | 1 | D6-4 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
| 11-20 | 1 | D6-3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 1 |

**Withdrawing Ships:** A player may also choose to withdraw ships to get them fully repaired at a major base. Mark the fact they have been withdrawn on the fleet register. Ships which are withdrawn are unavailable for the player's next game, after which they return to the fleet with their full number of hits. Escort squadrons which are withdrawn may return at full strength.

*Note: Vessels that begin a campaign game crippled must be damaged at least once during the game to count as crippled for the purpose of victory points.*

## 6. Appeals

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, printed p.135.*

After repairs have been completed, both players can appeal to higher authorities and/or the gods of Chaos for aid. The amount of help you can expect to receive depends on how well you've been doing, as measured by your renown. To reflect this, the number of appeals that may be made depend on the players' renown as shown on the table below.

| Renown | No. of Appeals |
|---|---|
| 1-10 | 1 Appeal |
| 11-30 | 2 Appeals |
| 31-50 | 3 Appeals |
| 51+ | 4 Appeals |

Appeals may normally only be made for refits and reinforcements. If you are allowed to make more than one appeal you can ask for the same thing up to two times (and may have each appeal granted), or you can appeal for different things, it's up to you! However, you must declare what you will appeal for this turn before determining whether the appeals have been granted. Having declared what you are going to appeal for, roll a D6 for each appeal to see if the appeal is granted.

| Appeal Type | Score Needed |
|---|---|
| Reinforcements | 2+ |
| Refits | 4+ |
| Other | 5+ |

*Note: Some campaigns may allow access to other types of appeals besides refits and reinforcements. These will naturally use the "Other" category of the table above. However, raider fleets may only ever appeal for refits and/or reinforcements, regardless of the campaign being played.*

**Reinforcements.** If the appeal is granted, one new capital ship or a squadron of up to five escorts may be added to the player's fleet registry. Note that you must have the models to represent the ships, if you don't, then they can't be added to the fleet register.

**Refits.** A player who is granted a refit must choose one of his capital ships to undergo the refit, then roll a D6 to see what type of equipment system is upgraded. On a roll of 1 or 2 you receive a ship refit, on a roll of 3 or 4 an engine refit, and on a roll of 5 or 6 a weapon refit. If a ship already has every refit in the rolled category, roll again until you get a different category.

The ship, engine, and weapon refit tables are in the Appendix (Appendix 2, p.147). Always use the table that matches your faction list's source book: Imperial Refits for the Imperium, Chaos, the Inquisition, Rogue Traders, the Adeptus Mechanicus and the Space Marines; Xenos Refits for the xenos fleets. For example, Chaos ships that earn a weapon refit roll on the Weapon Refit section of the Imperial Refits table.

If you roll a result that the ship already has, roll again until you get a refit the ship does not have. The points value of the ship is increased by 10% for each refit it has and you'll need to update your ship register appropriately.

**Other Appeals.** Other appeals typically allow you to request the aid of other allies or beseech your gods for a miracle. As before, if you roll a result on the table that you already have, then roll again until you get a new result.

*Flavour text:* "The best way of improving a gun is to improve its ammunition."

As noted in the introduction, players should set a deadline for the campaign. When the deadline is reached, the player that has built up the greatest renown is the winner. However, once you've got some experience running campaigns like this, you should feel free to change the criteria for victory.

## Sub-Sector Maps

*Source: BFG:XR Rulebook, printed p.138.*

**Number of Systems.** A sub-sector contains many stars, but of these only a few will have any planets orbiting, the vast majority of which will be gas giants or planets locked in sub-zero ice ages. This means that any given sub-sector will have relatively few star systems actually worth fighting over. Of these, the majority will be mining worlds, agri-worlds and other worlds with a sizable population and contemporary level of technology (categorized as civilized worlds). A few systems may have a forge world, hive world or other such planet. Occasionally uninhabited systems also have strategic importance as jump points or gathering places for assembling war fleets. As a result of these factors, the sub-sector maps only show those systems of military or strategic importance to the forces fighting in the campaign.

**Warpspace Channels.** In theory it is possible to travel anywhere through warp space. However, the warp's shifting tides make it easier to travel from some systems to others, and short warp jumps are always more accurate than longer ones. This is particularly true when moving a large fleet, which may become spread out across several light years of space. For this reason, the systems on a sub-sector map are connected by a number of warpspace channels (also known as warp routes) to the other systems.

**System Type.** Each system will be one of the following types: uninhabited, agri-world, mining world, hive world, penal colony, forge world or civilized world, as shown in the map key. Note that a system may actually contain more than one world, but the political power and the bulk of the resources will be concentrated on the type of world shown. The system type will influence how much ship damage it can repair and level of its orbital defenses.
