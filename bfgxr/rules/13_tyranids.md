# Tyranids (Xenos p.104-120)

## Faction Rules

Source book: *BFG:XR - Ships of the Xenos*, "The Tyranids in Battlefleet Gothic", "Tyranid Special Rules" and "Evolution of the Hive Mind". Printed pp.104-109 (PDF pp.108-113). Reminder: for this book, printed page + 4 = PDF page.

### Instinctive Behaviour

All Tyranid vessels - except synapse creatures - must act as directed by the Instinctive Behaviour chart shown below unless the Hive Mind (i.e. you, the player!) tells them to do otherwise (see the Synaptic Control section below). For ships or squadrons acting on Instinctive Behaviour, read down the flow chart shown below and resolve the first possible action or special order that would apply. No command check is needed for special orders issued by Instinctive Behaviour, but there may be specific activities that must be undertaken in the vessel's movement.

| Does the ship meet this condition? | If yes, take action... |
|---|---|
| Normal movement will take the ship into a hazardous celestial phenomena (e.g. a gas cloud, asteroid field, warp rift, etc)? | *Burn Retros.* The ship must turn away from the hazard as much as possible. |
| Nearest enemy ship is in front fire arc and less than 15cm away? (You may skip this condition if the acting ship is armed with bio-plasma). | No special order. The ship must move into base contact with the enemy and declare a boarding action, if possible. |
| Nearest enemy ship is in front fire arc and more than 60cm away? | *All Ahead Full.* |
| Nearest enemy ship is in rear fire arc? This condition only applies if the acting vessel is an escort ship. | *Come to New Heading.* |
| An enemy ship is in front fire arc and within range/fire arc of operational bio-weapons? | *Lock On.* |
| Ordnance requires reloading? | *Reload Ordnance.* |
| None of the previous conditions apply? | No special order. The ship must try to move closer to a planet or synapse creature. If it cannot, move half-speed straight ahead. |

Note that the effects of Instinctive Behaviour mean that Tyranid ships do not roll for leadership values - they will either have a fixed value themselves or use the value of a synapse creature in range. If a ship or squadron is forced to take a leadership test and no leadership value is specified, it uses a nominal leadership value of 7.

**Synaptic Control:** Some ships in your fleet are called *synapse creatures*; these ships project the psychic will of the Hive Mind throughout the fleet. Each turn before moving, a synapse creature can attempt to exert the Hive Mind's control over one or more units (including itself, if necessary). To do so, choose a ship or squadron within the creature's *synapse range* and make a command check. If successful, the target ignores Instinctive Behaviour for that turn and may be placed on any special order without requiring a second command check. The synapse creature may then choose a new target and repeat this process as many times as desired until it runs out of valid targets or fails a command check.

This ability *replaces* the usual method of issuing special orders (meaning a synapse creature must receive successful synaptic control from itself or another synapse creature if it wants to go on special orders, even though it does not have any Instinctive Behaviour to override).

Note that failing a command check does not prevent any other synapse creatures from using synaptic control, nor does it prevent the target from receiving successful synaptic control later in the turn (a single unit may only benefit once per turn, however). Lastly, Tyranid ordnance is not subject to synaptic control or Instinctive Behaviour - just move them like normal ordnance.

**Brace for Impact:** During either player's turn, Tyranid ships/squadrons may go onto *Brace for Impact* orders by testing against the leadership value of the nearest synapse creature within range. If no synapse creatures are within range, then the ship uses a nominal leadership value of 7. As normal, this order is only removed at the end of the Tyranids' next turn.

Ships on *Brace For Impact* orders at the start of their turn may still be directed by synaptic control but may not be placed on any special orders. If left to follow Instinctive Behaviour, they must obey any movement restrictions or required actions, but do not change their special orders.

### Navigation, Shooting and Spores

**Navigation:** All Tyranid ships are naturally adapted void swimming organisms and will thus attempt all leadership checks to navigate celestial phenomena counting as Leadership 10.

**Shooting:** All Tyranid direct fire weapons must shoot at either the closest enemy ship within range and firing arc, or a target that has been 'highlighted' by a vessel with Pheromone Sprayers (with no target priority test needed or allowed). Note that this means ordnance which is not highlighted cannot be fired upon at all.

**Spores:** As living organisms, Tyranid vessels do not have turrets or void shields in the traditional sense, and must instead rely on emitting a constantly replenished physical barrier of spore creatures to block hostile attacks. Each Tyranid ship profile lists a number of *spore cysts* for its Turrets value. When attacked by ordnance, spore cysts act just like normal turrets with the following exceptions:

- They can always be used against any type of ordnance without restriction.

- For each blast marker the ship is touching, one turret attack will only hit on a roll of 6+ (affected spore cysts must roll to hit in a separate group).

In addition, a ship's spore cysts produce a *spore cloud* (as shown in its Shields value). Such vessels count as having a number of shields equal to their number of spore cysts. Furthermore, the spore cloud causes enemy ships to treat the entire base of the Tyranid vessel as a blast marker as long as they remain in contact. Note that massing spore cysts as turrets does *not* affect a ship's shield strength.

### Boarding and Hit & Run Attacks

**Boarding:** Tyranids are a horror in boarding actions. The fearless, animalistic rush of clawed, fanged monsters has been the death knell of many a fine ship. Tyranid vessels double their troop rating and roll 2D6 and choose the highest score for boarding action results. Furthermore, Tyranid ships never count as being in contact with blast markers for the purposes of enemy boarding action bonuses.

**Hit & Run Attacks:** The nightmarish innards of a bio-ship are an environment hostile enough to rival the worst death-worlds. Because of this, all enemy hit-and-run attacks against Tyranid ships subtract 1 from the result. Likewise, Tyranids are experts at breaching enemy defences and infesting the strongest redoubts - their ships and ordnance add +1 to their roll when conducting hit-and-run raids.

Note that although they do not have teleporters in the typical sense, Tyranid ships may still attempt hit-and-run teleport attacks in the end phase just like the ships of other races, representing small groups of space-borne Tyranids striking out against nearby enemies of opportunity.

### Tyranid Ordnance

Tyranid attack craft are not subject to the normal limit on launching squadrons, due to their ability to continuously spawn new bio-fighters and other terrifying creatures. A Tyranid fleet may have up to *twice* the number of attack craft in play as they have available launch glands.

**Antibodies:** Tyranid antibodies are slow but numerous multi-role attack craft. They have a speed of 15cm and combine the abilities of fighters and assault boats. The rules for multi-role attack craft can be found in the main rulebook.

**Hull-borers:** Hull-borers follow the rules for boarding torpedoes, and have a speed of 20cm.

**Phage Mines:** Phage mines function just like regular mines, except that each hit against a ship's hull (not its shields) causes one Fire critical hit rather than inflicting a point of damage (regardless of the target's critical hit table). When armed with phage mines, a ship's launch bays also count as mine launchers. Phage mines do not replace a ship's ordnance when purchased.

### Tyranid Weapons

**Bio-Plasma:** Bio-plasma follows the normal rules for lance weapons, but due to its slow speed it will pass through a target's shields - such defences have no effect on hits from a bio-plasma attack (holofields and other defences will work as normal, however). Unfortunately, the slow speed of a bio-plasma shot also means that enemy ships have more time to evade - targets more than 15cm away can only be hit by bio-plasma on a D6 roll of 6.

**Feeder Tendrils:** When a Tyranid ship with feeder tendrils moves into base contact with an enemy vessel, it *must* make 1 roll to-hit per feeder tendril against that ship's lowest Armour value. For each hit, the target suffers 1 damage and D3 hit-and-run raids. These attacks always ignore a target's shields but cannot cause normal critical hits.

Once used, feeder tendrils cannot be used again in the same turn. Furthermore, feeder tendrils are not affected by special orders (though damage and hit-and-run results may be saved by bracing normally) and cannot be used at all if the equipped ship is crippled.

**Massive Claws:** When a Tyranid ship with massive claws moves into base contact with an enemy, it *must* make one roll to-hit for each point of massive claw strength. Claw attacks always hit on a roll of 4+, and if ramming the target when contact is made, you may re-roll any results that miss. Each hit inflicts one point of damage (ignoring shields).

Once used, massive claws cannot be used again in the same turn. Furthermore, massive claws are not affected by special orders (though damage may be saved by bracing normally) and cannot be used at all if the equipped ship is crippled.

If at least two hits are scored (and the target survives), the Tyranid vessel will *grapple* the enemy. When a Tyranid vessel begins grappling, it immediately comes to a halt. Note that if it has not yet moved its minimum speed when this occurs, it cannot board the enemy on that turn.

Each Tyranid vessel with massive claws can only grapple one enemy at a time, though this does not prevent several ships from grappling the same target. Grappling vessels (both Tyranids and their targets) cannot roll to disengage and must move according to the following rules:

- If it is grappling with at least one larger ship, it cannot move at all (and has no minimum speed).

- If it is grappling with at least one ship of equal size, it may only move up to half its speed.

- If it is only grappling with smaller ships, it reduces its speed by 5cm but otherwise moves normally.

In addition, grappling ships will 'carry' smaller vessels along with them, according to the following rules:

- After a Tyranid ship moves, pick up its grappled target (if any) and place it in base contact such that its abeam arc is touching the Tyranid vessel's front arc (rotating it as little as possible from its original heading).

- After a grappled ship moves, pick up each Tyranid ship grappling it and place them so that their front arcs are touching the grappled ship's abeam arc(s).

A ship can only be 'carried' this way once per turn, and carried ships do not themselves drag along other vessels. Any ships that cannot be placed back into base contact (for any reason) cease to be grappling.

If a Tyranid vessel begins its move already grappling an enemy, it must attack the grappled target with its claws (even if other targets are in base contact). If at least two hits are scored, it continues grappling (but does not halt its movement). Otherwise, it releases the target and must move away normally.

Grappling ships are treated as part of the same squadron for the purposes of either player's shooting. This means that attacks must be resolved against the closest ship(s) first, regardless of which vessels are friend or foe.

**Pyro-Acid Batteries:** Pyro-acid batteries follow the normal rules for gunnery weapons. In addition, ships that suffer a critical hit from a pyro-acid battery automatically suffer an additional Fire critical result as well, representing the flood of corrosive chemicals eating through entire decks and liquefying the unfortunate inhabitants.

**Arming Tyranid Ships:** Tyranid ships are much more flexible than the ships of other races, as new designs are constantly being evolved and encountered by the Imperium. To represent this, a Tyranid player can choose certain elements of their ships themselves. Each ship is limited as to how many weapon choices it may have for a given location, but where more than one selection is permitted you may select multiples of the same weapon system unless otherwise specified.

### Damage Tables

Tyranids are so wholly alien that conventional concepts of damage and critical systems do not apply. Rather than overloading reactors and decompressing compartments, a Tyranid bioship will quite literally bleed into the void just like a man bleeds from an open wound. As such, Tyranids use the Tyranid Critical Hits Table and Tyranid Catastrophic Damage Table (Appendix 3, p.227) instead of the normal tables in the main rulebook. As always, if a result is rolled which cannot be applied, apply the next highest critical result instead.

### Scenario Special Rules

The Tyranids are unlike any other race in the galaxy, and are not driven by greed, vengeance, or madness. Instead, they are propelled forth by the unfathomable will of the Hive Mind, their gestalt consciousness, which compels them to feed and grow to the exclusion of almost all else. As such, the Tyranids are subject to the following rules:

**Planetary Defences:** Whenever a Tyranid fleet defends in a scenario allowing planetary defence units, it may grant up to one in every ten escort drones the special rules for a Fire Ship (found in the main rulebook). In all other regards, the Tyranids are limited to using the planetary defences found in their faction list (they cannot field generic support units).

**Cruiser Clash:** It is uncommon for the Tyranids to travel with only a few cruisers (and bringing such a force would make for very boring games). As such, they ignore the usual rules and restrictions for fleet composition in a Cruiser Clash (as well as those in the fleet list itself), instead spending up to 750 points on capital ships. No Proteus hive ships or escorts may be taken, and the fleet may have no more than one Charybdis hive ship. Lastly, no upgrades or re-rolls may be purchased either.

**Convoy:** Tyranids, being a race of space-borne organisms, have no need for trade or convoys. Indeed, they have little reason to move goods or cargo anywhere except their bellies! As such, Tyranids must always be the attacker in a Convoy (or equivalent) scenario.

**Planetary Assault:** This is the classic Tyranid scenario - an attempt to invade and subdue a populated world (although they may also attempt to defend a recently conquered world as well). In this or any similar scenario, the Tyranid fleet ignores the transport capacity requirement when attacking. Instead, each hive ship, bio-cruiser, and escort drone is treated as having a transport capacity equal to its number of spore cysts.

**Escalating Engagement:** One of the few effective strategies against the Tyranids relies on exploiting the relatively slow speed of Tyranid vessels to break apart their core formation, then isolating the fragments and destroying them as quickly as possible. To counteract this, the Tyranids generally travel in huge, dense shoals. Tyranids therefore add +1 to the arrival roll of their divisions in an Escalating Engagement scenario, representing the close proximity of neighbouring forces.

**Exterminatus:** The Tyranids would never waste the valuable resources of a world by destroying it outright! As such, they have no reason to be the attacker in an Exterminatus scenario - they must always be the defender.

### Tyranid Biomorphs

These refits can be incorporated by any capital ships and/or escort squadrons in your fleet, except when specifically noted otherwise (see the fleet lists for details). Where two costs are shown, the first is the cost for capital ships, and the second is the cost *per escort* for an escort squadron. If the cost is a percentage, it is calculated against the *base cost* of the capital ship or escort squadron, rounded up to the nearest 5 points.

No ship or squadron may have more than one copy of any single biomorph. Furthermore, the number of biomorphs that a ship or squadron may purchase is limited by the size of the ship(s) in question:

- Hive ships may have up to three biomorphs.

- Other capital ships may have up to two biomorphs.

- Escort squadrons may only have one biomorph.

**Campaigns:** These bio-enhancements represent the adaptive qualities of the Tyranid Hive Fleets. As such, the Tyranids cannot have any other type of refits. During a campaign, no capital ship or escort squadron may gain more than one new biomorph before each battle. Moreover, Tyranids cannot gain crew skills of any kind. In addition, if a refit would be earned during a campaign, roll 2D6 and apply the bio-enhancement from the table below which corresponds with the score rolled, rather than using the regular refit tables. You may also use this method to generate biomorphs during a normal game if desired. Re-roll any result that cannot be applied.

### Tyranid Ship Special Rules

#### Proteus Class Hive Ship (Xenos p.110)

**Synapse Creature:** A Proteus class hive ship is a massive organism capable of instructing tens of thousands of Tyranids at once. It has a leadership value of 10 and a synapse range of 60cm.

**Special Rules:** A Proteus hive ship is extremely ponderous; it moves according to the rules for battleships, cannot use *Come to New Heading* special orders (regardless of any biomorphs) and only adds +2D6cm to its speed (rather than +4D6cm) when on *All Ahead Full* special orders. In addition, when suffering a critical hit to the thorax, roll a D6. On a 1-3, the damage applies to its keel weapons; on a 4-6, the damage applies to its dorsal weapons (keep track of dorsal and keel damage separately). Lastly, each 'spore cysts injured' critical hit only reduces its spore cysts by 1 instead of nullifying them completely.

**Phage Mines:** A Proteus hive ship's launch glands may be armed with phage mines for +60 points.

#### Charybdis Class Hive Ship (Xenos p.111)

**Synapse Creature:** A Charybdis class hive ship is standard Tyranid command-ship equivalent. It has a leadership value of 8 and a synapse range of 45cm.

**Special Rules:** A Charybdis hive ship is ponderous; it cannot use *Come to New Heading* special orders unless it has *both* solar vanes and extra discharge vents.

**Weapon Configuration:** A Charybdis class hive ship must be configured as follows, at the time you prepare your fleet list. The cost of each weapon is added to the cost of the hive ship, just like any other refit. It *must* choose one prow and one thorax weapon, and may have up to three total choices from the port and starboard weapon options. (You may select the same choice more than once). Where the ship has multiple copies of the same weapon in the same location, combine the weapons' firepower or strength together and treat it as a single weapon system.

- *Prow (choose one):* Pyro-acid Battery (Range 45cm, Firepower 8, Front) +30; Excretion Ducts (Hull-borers: 20cm, Strength 8, Front) +25; Bio-plasma Spines (Range 30cm, FP4, Front) +20; Massive Claws (Base Contact, Strength 3) +15.

- *Thorax (choose one):* Pyro-acid Battery (Range 45cm, Firepower 8, Front) +30; Bio-plasma Spines (Range 30cm, FP4, Front) +20; Launch Glands (Antibodies: 15cm, 2 Squadrons) +20.

- *Port / Starboard (up to three selections), +20 each:* Launch Glands: Port (Antibodies: 15cm, 1 Squadron), Starboard (Antibodies: 15cm, 1 Squadron). Pyro-acid Batteries: Port (Range 30cm, Firepower 4, Left), Starboard (Range 30cm, Firepower 4, Right). Bio-plasma Discharges: Port (Range 15cm, FP2, Left), Starboard (Range 15cm, FP2, Right).

*Xenos p.111 prints the starboard pyro-acid battery's range as 45cm. This is a typo for 30cm, matching the port battery.*

**Phage Mines:** A Charybdis' launch glands may be armed with phage mines for +5 points per point of their strength. For example, a hive ship with thorax launch glands and two sets of port/starboard launch glands would pay +30 points for phage mines.

#### Tyranid Bio-Cruiser (Xenos pp.112-113)

**Gene Template:** Each bio-cruiser starts with the profile and characteristics shown in the table above. It must then select one of the following *gene templates* which will determine its specific adaptations and role within the fleet.

- *Guardian (Free):* -1 Spore Cyst. Gains the Living Barrier special rule (see Escort Drone). Must pick thorax excretion ducts, bio-plasma spines, or pyro-acid battery. Must pick any one port / starboard weapon set.

- *Scylla (+15 points):* Must pick any one prow weapon. Must pick thorax excretion ducts, bio-plasma spines, or pyro-acid battery. May choose up to two port / starboard weapon sets. May have a minor synapse node.

- *Prowler (+20 points):* -1 Spore Cyst. Gains the Pheromone Sprayers special rule (see Vanguard Drone). Must pick prow pyro-acid battery or feeder tendrils. Must pick thorax pyro-acid battery or feeder tendrils.

- *Carnivore (+25 points):* Replace starting prow feeder tendrils with massive claws (Base Contact, Strength 2). Gains the Bonded Exo-plating special rule (see Kraken). Must pick prow excretion ducts, massive claws, or feeder tendrils. Must pick two *different* thorax weapon options.

**Weapon Configuration:** Each bio-cruiser must be configured as described in its chosen gene template, at the time you prepare your fleet list. The cost of each weapon is added to the cost of the bio-cruiser, just like any other refit. Where the vessel has multiple copies of the same weapon in the same location, combine the weapons' firepower or strength together and treat it as a single weapon system. Note that for the Port and Starboard Weapons section, each selection provides the chosen weapon on both the port and starboard side, firing Left and Right, respectively.

- *Prow:* Excretion Ducts (Hull-borers: 20cm, Strength 4, Front) 15; Massive Claws (Base Contact, Strength 2) 10; Pyro-acid Battery (Range: 30cm, Firepower 4, Left / Front / Right) 10; Feeder Tendrils (Base Contact, Strength 1) 5.

- *Thorax:* Excretion Ducts (Hull-borers: 20cm, Strength 4, Front) 15; Bio-plasma Spines (Range: 30cm, FP2, Front) 10; Pyro-acid Battery (Range: 30cm, Firepower 4, Left / Front / Right) 10; Feeder Tendrils (Base Contact, Strength 1) 5.

- *Port and Starboard:* Pyro-acid Batteries (Range: 30cm, Firepower 4) 20; Bio-plasma Discharges (Range: 15cm, FP2) 20; Launch Glands (1 squadron each side; Antibodies: 15cm) 20.

**Phage Mines:** A bio-cruiser with launch glands may upgrade them to be armed with phage mines for +5 points per gland strength. For example, a Scylla with two sets of port and starboard launch glands would pay +20 points for phage mines.

**Minor Synapse Node:** A Scylla strain bio-cruiser may be given a minor synapse node for +5 points, making it a synapse creature with Leadership 7 and 30cm synapse range. Note that this causes the vessel to be considered a hive ship for the purpose of biomorph limits, but for all other purposes it uses the type indicated in the fleet list for which it is taken (e.g. it remains a bio-cruiser in a Hive Fleet list, but acts as a primary hive ship in the Gestating Fleet list).

#### Kraken (Xenos p.114)

**Bonded Exo-plating:** The vessel has a shell of fused, chitinous plates, making it capable of sustaining unthinkable amounts of damage. It is treated as having 6+ Armour and gains a 4+ save against incoming attacks (usable any time a *Brace for Impact* save could be used). However, it also loses all of its spore cysts and is unaffected by *Brace for Impact* special orders - it must rely entirely on its sealed carapace to survive.

**Weapon Configuration:** A kraken must be configured as follows, at the time you prepare your fleet list. The cost of each weapon is added to the cost of the vessel. Each kraken in the same squadron *must* have the same weapon. Choose one: Pyro-acid Battery (Range 30cm, Firepower 6, Front) +15; Bio-plasma Discharge (Range 15cm, FP2, Front) +10; Excretion Ducts (Hull-borers: 20cm, Strength 2, Front) +10; Massive Claws (Base Contact, Strength 2) +10; Feeder Tendrils (Base Contact, Strength 1) +5.

#### Vanguard Drone (Xenos p.114)

**Pheromone Sprayers:** The vessel is adapted to emit a mist of biosensor-reactive pheromones in the area around itself, "highlighting" nearby vessels as threats to other Tyranids in the vicinity. Enemy ships and ordnance within 15cm of a ship with this special rule may be targeted freely by other Tyranids in the fleet (no target priority test needed).

**Weapon Configuration:** A vanguard must be configured as follows, at the time you prepare your fleet list. Each vanguard in the same squadron *must* have the same weapon. Choose one: Pyro-acid Battery (Range 30cm, Firepower 4, Front), Free; Feeder Tendrils (Base Contact, Strength 1), Free.

#### Escort Drone (Xenos p.115)

**Living Barrier:** The vessel is especially good at using its own body as a shield, protecting more valuable elements of the fleet at its own expense. If an enemy wishes to fire upon a Tyranid capital ship that is behind the base of at least one ship within 15cm with this special rule, it must pass its target priority test with a -1Ld penalty. This effect does not stack, and an enemy may choose not to shoot at the protected vessel(s) in order to avoid suffering this penalty.

**Slow Gestation:** Due to their size, escort drones are slow to grow new hull-borers. As a result, while escort drones armed with excretion ducts are on *Reload Ordnance* special orders, the strength of their excretion ducts is halved.

**Weapon Configuration:** An escort drone must be configured as follows, at the time you prepare your fleet list. Each escort drone in the same squadron *must* have the same weapon. Choose one, all Free: Pyro-acid Battery (Range 30cm, Firepower 4, Front); Bio-plasma Discharge (Range 15cm, FP2, Front); Excretion Ducts (Hull-borers: 20cm, Strength 2, Front); Feeder Tendrils (Base Contact, Strength 1).

#### Infested Space Station (Xenos p.116)

An infested space station is a planetary defence choice for any Tyranid fleet, and will follow the rules for orbital stations as described in the main rulebook. Due to the presence of genestealers and genestealer hybrids, an infested space station doubles its troop rating during a boarding action, and rolls 2D6 and picks the highest score for its boarding action result. Furthermore, it adds +1 to the result of its hit-and-run attacks, and enemy hit-and-run attacks against it must subtract 1 from their score. However, since it is not actually a Tyranid station, it will *not* follow any other Tyranid special rules regarding boarding, hit-and-run attacks, instinctive behaviour, shields, shooting, and so forth. Finally, it may not be given any biomorphs or other refits.

#### Spore Field and Orbital Defence Spores (Xenos p.117)

**Spore Field:** A spore field follows all the normal rules for minefields as described in the main rulebook, except that in all cases it will generate phage mines rather than normal mines, including when it attacks a detected ship inside the field.

**Orbital Defence Spores:** Orbital defence spores are ordnance markers which follow the rules for phage mines in all respects, in addition to being planetary defences. They are deployed just like orbital mines (found in the standard Fleet Support list) at the start of the game. *Antibodies:* Any orbital defence spores in your fleet may be upgraded to function as Tyranid Antibodies (instead of phage mines) for +5 points each. They are still deployed like regular orbital mines, however.

**Fleet Composition (Hive, Vanguard and Gestating Fleet Lists)**

Three fleet lists (*Xenos pp.118-120*). None will ever provide ships to or take ships from any other fleet list as reserves or allies. In every list, ships may purchase biomorphs, and the cost of all biomorphs and weapon selections is always added to the corresponding ship's cost for the purpose of determining its victory point value. Tyranids do not have fleet commanders in the traditional sense; hive ships lead the fleet.

**Leadership & Instinctive Behaviour (Vanguard Fleet):** The vessels in a vanguard fleet must function on their own for extended periods. As such, they are subject to the following special rules:

- Prowler strain bio-cruisers have Ld7 and add +1Ld for each other Prowler in their squadron (max Ld10).

- Escort squadrons have a leadership value equal to the number of surviving ships in the squadron (min Ld6, max Ld10). Kraken count as two escorts each for this purpose.

- Before moving, each ship or squadron in the fleet may attempt a command check. If the check is passed, the ship or squadron ignores Instinctive Behaviour, exactly as if a hive ship used its synaptic control.

- All ships and squadrons use their own leadership when attempting *Brace for Impact* orders, counting as Ld7 if their normal leadership value is lower.
