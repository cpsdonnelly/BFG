# Dark Eldar (Xenos p.2-13)

## Faction Rules

Source book: *BFG:XR - Ships of the Xenos*, "The Dark Eldar in Battlefleet Gothic", "Dark Eldar Weapons", "Dark Eldar Upgrades" and "Critical Hits". Printed pp.4-6 (PDF pp.8-10). Reminder: for this book, printed page + 4 = PDF page.

**A Note on Eldar Rules:** Although they are technically part of the Eldar race, and share many technological similarities with their corsair and craftworld cousins, the Dark Eldar are a wholly separate faction that will only rarely work with others outside their domain. As such, the Dark Eldar do not normally use any of the special rules for Eldar Corsairs or Craftworld Eldar presented elsewhere. Where they do use a similar rule or special effect, it has been re-printed here to avoid any confusion.

**Leadership & Movement**

**Dark Eldar Leadership:** Dark Eldar are fiercely competitive and must continuously prove themselves, lest a subordinate find a weakness to exploit. Likewise, they must be wary of their superiors, so that their own scheming goes unnoticed. Dark Eldar vessels generate leadership in the normal way, but due to their fearsome nature, they add +1 to their leadership value, giving a range of 7-10.

**Movement:** Dark Eldar vessels are sleek and agile, bearing numerous arrays of delicate fins and sophisticated manoeuvring systems that make Imperial warships appear leaden by comparison. As such, Dark Eldar ships have no minimum speed and require no movement before they can turn. In addition, they automatically pass command checks to go on *Come to New Heading* special orders. Note that Dark Eldar ships will still be targeted as defences if they move less than 5cm during their turn.

### Slave-Taking, Greed and Frenzy

**Slave-Taking:** All Dark Eldar are prone to take captives, even when doing so is tactically foolish. When any Dark Eldar ship (or a squadron of its attack craft) conducts a hit-and-run attack, you may forgo rolling the result in exchange for immediately scoring +5 victory points. When conducting the special attack from an Impaler Assault Module, you may score D6x5 victory points instead of resolving any hit-and-run raids or critical hits.

Note that braced vessels still make a saving throw against slave-taking attacks (an Impaler engaged in slave-taking is always treated as a single attack for this purpose). If the save is passed, the attack earns no victory points.

Additionally, during a boarding action involving a Dark Eldar vessel, the Dark Eldar player scores an extra +5 victory points for each hit inflicted against the opposition. If a 'Bridge Smashed' critical hit is inflicted on an enemy during the boarding action, the Dark Eldar player gains +100 victory points as some of the opponent's command staff is captured. Other critical hit results, including extra damage and so forth, do not award any additional victory points.

**Insatiable Greed:** The boundless avarice and sadistic aggression displayed by the Dark Eldar belies the desperation with which they must acquire new captives to feed their decaying souls. As such, non-crippled Dark Eldar ships that disengage (for any reason except scenario victory conditions) count as destroyed unless their fleet has scored at least 25% of its own value in victory points.

**Pain Frenzy:** Dark Eldar are nigh-universally violent and sadistic. As such, Dark Eldar ships benefit from a +1 crew damage bonus in boarding actions and add +1 to the result of any hit-and-run raids they (or their ordnance) perform.

Additionally, Dark Eldar have a particular penchant for targeting the weak and vulnerable. Against enemy vessels that are crippled, Dark Eldar may re-roll the result of any hit-and-run raid, boarding critical hit, or Impaler critical hit they inflict (taking the second result).

### Spectral Plating and the Mimic Engine

**Spectral Plating:** Dark Eldar vessels are sheathed in interlocking layers of resin-based armour plates studded with reactive forcefield projectors that turn away errant stellar debris. As a result, Dark Eldar ships never risk suffering damage from blast markers or gas clouds, despite being unshielded.

**The Mimic Engine:** The mimic engine is an insidious invention that allows a Dark Eldar vessel to assume the appearance of another species' ship, thus allowing the Dark Eldar to sneak up on their prey unseen. All Dark Eldar ships are equipped with a mimic engine at no extra cost.

After deployment, but before either player has taken their first turn, each ship with a mimic engine may make one basic move up to 15cm. This does not allow a ship to go on special orders before the first turn.

In addition, until the end of the first game turn, a mimic engine-equipped ship that remains more than 30cm from any enemy vessel cannot be targeted by enemy shooting. This includes launching torpedoes at the ship, unless the path of the marker would first contact a different enemy target within 30cm. Otherwise, all contact with ordnance is still resolved normally.

If a mimicking ship fires any weapons, defends itself with turrets, launches any ordnance, or otherwise attacks in any way, it is immediately revealed. Revealed ships lose all benefits of their mimic engines and may be attacked normally. Against Necron and Tyranid fleets, mimic engines do not have any effect (before deployment or otherwise). The advanced sensory systems of their vessels cannot be fooled by such tricks!

### Shadowfields

Dark Eldar vessels are protected by a sophisticated ECM system that produces an eerie and uncertain fog around their ships, through which very little information can be ascertained. Pinpointing the exact location or course of a Dark Eldar vessel behind shadowfields can prove very arduous indeed. Shadowfields do *not* count as shields, but instead provide the following effects:

**Vs. Gunnery:** Against attacks which make use of the gunnery table, shadowfields force one additional right column-shift in addition to any other column shifts that apply.

In addition, after a ship or squadron with shadowfields is hit by a gunnery weapon, place a 'tracking' mark on that unit (limit once per target per attacking ship or squadron). These marks last until the end of the turn, and are applied even if the hit was saved by bracing. (If you do not have specific tokens on hand, use dice or coins to indicate the number of tracking marks on each unit).

**Vs. Lances & Ordnance:** Against hits (or hit-and-run raids) caused by lances and ordnance, shadowfields provide a 2+ save to represent the attacker failing to acquire its target behind the obscuring darkness. However, for each tracking mark on the unit (see above), the save difficulty increases by 1, to a maximum of 6+, as 'tracer' fire illuminates the ship's location.

**Vs. Ramming & Nova Cannon:** Leadership tests to ram ships protected by shadowfields are taken on an extra +2D6, due to the difficulty of setting an accurate trajectory. Likewise, nova cannon will scatter +1D6cm, even if a 'Hit' is shown on the scatter die (roll the scatter die again to determine the direction).

**Vs. Other Attacks:** Against boarding actions, teleport attacks, catastrophic damage effects, or any other attack type not listed above, shadowfields offer no protection of any kind.

### Dark Eldar Weapons

**Gunnery Weapons:** Due to their enormous volume of fire, any Dark Eldar weapon that uses the gunnery table gains an automatic left column-shift at all times. This is in addition to any other column-shifts that would apply.

**Phantom Lances:** A phantom lance functions exactly like a regular lance weapon, but scores one hit on a roll of 4, and two hits on a roll of 5+. For the purpose of saves and other defences, count each hit separately.

**All Ordnance:** All Dark Eldar torpedoes and attack craft are protected by flickerfields, an advanced type of optical force-field which interferes with enemy targeting systems by making it appear that the shielded object "flickers" in and out of existence. As such, enemy turrets add +2 to their hit requirement when rolling to hit Dark Eldar ordnance (except mines), to a maximum of 6+.

**Attack Craft:** Dark Eldar fighters are designed to operate far from their mothership, raiding planets even while their base remains at the edge of the system. As such, Raptor fighters have a 4+ resilient save. All other attack craft, such as Razorwing bombers, Slavebringer assault boats, and Voidraven torpedo bombers follow the normal rules for their type. Note that Voidravens launch plasma torpedoes (see below).

**Plasma Torpedoes:** Dark Eldar plasma torpedoes are designed to strip away hull and ignite the vulnerable corridors within their target. They are treated as standard torpedoes that must re-roll missed attacks.

**Leech Torpedoes:** Leech torpedoes are a unique Dark Eldar creation designed to slow down targets of a raiding party. Leech torpedoes roll to hit against an enemy vessel's Armour just like standard torpedoes, however instead of causing any damage, each hit results in an automatic 'Thrusters Damaged' critical result (even for ships that do not have a 'Thrusters Damaged' result on their critical hit table). Do not apply any extra damage from this result.

**Impaler Assault Modules:** The Impaler Assault Module is an extremely large assault boat used by the Dark Eldar. Mounted on the prow of their vessels, the Impaler carries literally hundreds of assault troops ready to board an enemy ship. As a special attack craft, a Dark Eldar player may not have more Impalers in play than the total Impaler launch capacity of his or her fleet, in the same way that other attack craft are limited by the fleet's launch bays. In addition, Impalers left on the table at the start of the owning player's turn are automatically removed due to lack of fuel.

An Impaler has a resilient save of 4+ due to its size and substantial defence systems. When it contacts an enemy vessel, roll a D6. On a result of 1, the attack has no effect - the module fails to connect with its target. Otherwise, the module inflicts two hit-and-run attacks or a single critical hit on the enemy (attacker's choice). In addition, if the initial D6 roll equals or exceeds the target's Armour value, the attack also inflicts one point of damage (before resolving other effects), representing the Impaler's bladed prow carving through the target's hull. After resolving an Impaler module's attack, the marker is removed as normal. (Bracing applies separately to each effect).

### Dark Eldar Upgrades

The ships of the Dark Eldar are filled from stem to stern with some of the most twisted and deranged killers the galaxy has ever seen. They may be given the following upgrades as indicated in their fleet list (costs are in the Dark Eldar Raiders Fleet List):

- *Incubi:* Some particularly paranoid Archons will hire contingents of Incubi warriors as bodyguards. When a ship carrying Incubi (or any ordnance it has launched) conducts a hit-and-run raid, roll 2D6 and choose one result to apply. If the target ship is crippled, instead apply *both* results (this replaces the re-roll normally provided by the Pain Frenzy special rule). In addition, enemy hit-and-run raids targeting a ship with Incubi suffer a -1 penalty.

- *Wych Cults:* Sometimes a Wych Cult will be hired to supplement the Archon's ship-to-ship warriors. A Dark Eldar vessel with a Wych Cult doubles its troop rating. In addition, the ship and its ordnance count all enemy capital ships as crippled for the purpose of Pain Frenzy and boarding modifiers.

- *Haemonculi Coven:* Groups of Haemonculi are a particularly useful asset to an Archon. When a Dark Eldar vessel with Haemonculi inflicts a critical hit via a boarding action or an Impaler module, resolve the effect as a 'Fire' result instead of rolling randomly, to represent hordes of wild grotesques and other monstrosities loosed upon the target ship. In addition, a vessel with Haemonculi gains +1 hit point, as the new crew has become accustomed to pain.

### Dark Eldar Ship Special Rules

#### Mortalis Class Grand Cruiser (Xenos p.7)

**Kabal Variants:** Each Mortalis class grand cruiser you include *must* select one (and only one) of the following options when assembling your fleet. Any mix of variants may be taken as desired:

- *Obsidian Rose Pattern:* The vessel gains a prow phantom lance (Range: 30cm, FP3, Front) for no change in cost.

- *Iron Thorn Pattern:* The vessel gains a keel launch bay (6 Squadrons) equipped with Raptor fighters (Speed: 30cm), Razorwing bombers (Speed: 20cm), and Slavebringer assault boats (Speed: 30cm) for +20 points. For an additional +60 points, the vessel may either take Voidraven torpedo bombers or replace its launch bay with a keel mine launcher (Strength 6).

- *Dying Sun Pattern:* The vessel gains a prow impaler bay (Speed: 30cm, 3 Modules) for no change in cost.

- *Falling Moon Pattern:* The vessel gains a keel torpedo salvo (Speed: 30cm, Strength 6, Front) equipped with plasma, leech, and boarding torpedoes for no change in cost.

#### Torture Class Cruiser (Xenos p.8)

**Kabal Variants:** Each Torture class cruiser you include *must* select one (and only one) of the following options when assembling your fleet. Any mix of variants may be taken as desired:

- *Flayed Skull Pattern:* The vessel gains a prow phantom lance (Range: 30cm, FP2, Front) for no change in cost.

- *Fiend Ascendant Pattern (+20pts):* The vessel gains a keel launch bay (4 Squadrons) equipped with Raptor fighters (Speed: 30cm), Razorwing bombers (Speed: 20cm), and Slavebringer assault boats (Speed: 30cm) for +20 points. For an additional +40 points, the vessel may either take Voidraven torpedo bombers or replace its launch bay with a keel mine launcher (Strength 4).

- *Bleaksoul Pattern:* The vessel gains a prow impaler bay (Speed: 30cm, 2 Modules) for no change in cost.

- *Bloodied Claw Pattern:* The vessel gains a keel torpedo salvo (Speed: 30cm, Strength 4, Front) equipped with plasma, leech, and boarding torpedoes for no change in cost.

#### Succubus Class Light Cruiser (Xenos p.9)

**Kabal Variants:** Each Succubus class light cruiser you include *must* select one (and only one) of the following options when assembling your fleet. Any mix of variants may be taken as desired:

- *Dark Mirror Pattern:* The vessel gains a prow phantom lance (Range: 30cm, FP2, Front) for no change in cost.

- *Burning Scale Pattern (+10pts):* The vessel gains a keel launch bay (2 Squadrons) equipped with Raptor fighters (Speed: 30cm), Razorwing bombers (Speed: 20cm), and Slavebringer assault boats (Speed: 30cm) for +10 points. For an additional +20 points, the vessel may either take Voidraven torpedo bombers or replace its launch bay with a keel mine launcher (Strength 2).

- *Baleful Gaze Pattern (+10pts):* The vessel gains a prow impaler bay (Speed: 30cm, 2 Modules) for +10 points.

- *Bladed Lotus Pattern (+10pts):* The vessel gains a keel torpedo salvo (Speed: 30cm, Strength 4, Front) equipped with plasma, leech, and boarding torpedoes for +10 points.

#### Corsair Class Frigate (Xenos p.10)

**Kabal Variants:** Each Corsair class frigate you include *must* select one (and only one) of the following options when assembling your fleet. Any mix of variants may be taken as desired. Corsair frigates equipped with a torpedo salvo are armed with plasma, leech, and boarding torpedoes.

- Phantom lance (Range: 30cm, FP1, Front): Free

- Impaler bay (Speed: 30cm, 1 Module): Free

- Torpedo salvo (Speed: 30cm, Strength 2, Front): Free

**Kabal Variants:** Each Subjugation class raider you include *must* select one (and only one) of the following options when assembling your fleet. Any mix of variants may be taken as desired. Subjugation frigates equipped with a torpedo salvo are armed with plasma, leech, and boarding torpedoes.

- Weapons battery (Range: 30cm, Firepower 3, Front): Free

#### Dark Eldar Transport (Xenos p.11)

A Dark Eldar transport is a convoy ship and will follow all the normal rules for fleet support units (and convoy ships) described in the main rulebook. It has a transport capacity of 1, and will benefit from all applicable Dark Eldar special rules. Dark Eldar transports may *only* be included when the chosen scenario imposes a transport capacity requirement on your fleet.

#### Tormentor Class Spire (Xenos pp.11-12)

A Tormentor Spire is a high orbit defence choice for any Dark Eldar fleet. It follows the standard rules for fleet support units as described in the main rulebook, except where modified below. A Tormentor Spire's torpedo launcher is equipped with plasma, leech, and boarding torpedoes.

**Leadership:** A Tormentor has a nominal leadership value of 8. However, a fleet's commander may lead the battle aboard the Tormentor (even if normally forced to embark somewhere else), allowing it to use his or her leadership instead.

**Night Shields:** The Tormentor Spire is equipped with an advanced form of optical camouflage similar to that used on Dark Eldar skimmers. Any enemy ship targeting a Tormentor counts as being 10cm further away than it actually is for the purposes of determining range and effects of all shooting and teleport attacks.

**Polarization Field:** A low-level energy bubble surrounds the Tormentor Spire, channeling space debris away from and around the station. The station never risks suffering a hit for being in contact with blast markers and ignores all effects of solar flares. This replaces the 'Spectral Plating' special rule.

**Hidden Base:** A Tormentor, like many Dark Eldar outposts, relies more on its ability to remain hidden than any capacity to repulse a sustained assault. As a result, Tormentor Spires *cannot* be used in any battlezone from the Flare Region to the Inner Biosphere.

When playing in a campaign, a Dark Eldar fleet may start with a free Tormentor Spire in their fleet list, and may earn up to two more via special appeal roll (note that any that are lost still count against this limit). To do so, they must have 11+ renown and score 5+ on the appeal roll to succeed. Each Tormentor Spire on a player's campaign fleet list counts as a hidden base (see the Build-Up section of the campaign rules). In individual battles, the cost of any Tormentor Spire that participates must still be counted normally as part of the chosen forces.

When operating as a hidden base, if a Tormentor is revealed and survives the initial attack, the owning player can attempt to retreat the station back into the Webway to prevent further assaults on it. To do so, the owner rolls a D6 after the battle. On a roll of 1 the Tormentor Spire is lost in the Webway and must be struck from the roster. On a 2+, it has successfully traversed the Webway to a new system and must once again be located by an opponent before it can be attacked.

**Docking:** Dark Eldar ships can dock (or undock) with a Tormentor Spire in base contact by passing a leadership test. Ships that are docked cannot attack or launch ordnance, though shadowfields continue to work normally, and they can attempt special orders if desired. Vessels that remain docked with the station for one full turn gain +2D6 to repair critical damage in the end phase, and are considered to have reloaded ordnance for free. The station may also apply its repair bonus to itself if there are no ships docked with it at any point during its turn.

**Webway Portal:** Many Dark Eldar Tormentors are equipped with a Webway Portal connecting directly to Commorragh. The owning player may use this link to summon additional warships to its aid during a battle. If the fleet includes a Tormentor, it may take Dark Eldar vessels as reserves at 50% of their normal cost. However, any such ships *must* start the game off the table. Beginning on turn 2, after the Dark Eldar fleet moves, roll a D6. On a 5+, one reserve capital ship or escort squadron (owner's choice) appears along the table edge closest to the Tormentor Spire. These reserve ships cannot move in the same turn they arrive, and do not benefit from their mimic engines.

**Only the Strong May Lead (optional rule):** At the start of the game, if a Dracon's vessel has a higher leadership value than the Dread Archon's vessel, that Dracon will attempt to seize control of the fleet. Roll a D6 for the Dread Archon and each qualifying Dracon, and add their respective leadership values to their results. If the Dread Archon's roll is higher, he maintains order and nothing happens. Otherwise, the Dracon with the best roll successfully wins the loyalty of the fleet. If this happens, that Dracon becomes the new Dread Archon (gaining +1Ld and inheriting any re-rolls purchased), and the previous Dread Archon is replaced by a Dracon (suffering -1Ld and losing any re-rolls purchased).
